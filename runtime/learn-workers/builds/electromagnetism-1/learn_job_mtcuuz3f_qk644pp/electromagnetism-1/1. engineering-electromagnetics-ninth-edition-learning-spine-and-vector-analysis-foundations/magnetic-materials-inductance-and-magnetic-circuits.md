# 1.372 Magnetic Materials, Inductance, and Magnetic Circuits

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 598, Index entries: Electron spin, Energy, Ferrimagnetic materials, Ferromagnetic materials, Page 599, Index entries: Hysteresis, Inductance, Internal inductance, Page 600, Index entries: Magnetic materials, Magnetic moments, Magnetization, Mutual inductance, Page 601, Index entries: Permeability, Potential energy and forces on magnetic materials, Reluctance, Page 602, Index entries: Self-inductance, Superparamagnetic materials

The magnetic-material entries connect atomic magnetic moments to macroscopic magnetization, permeability, energy, force, and circuit models. Electron spin, orbiting electrons, nuclear spin, and magnetic moments appear as microscopic origins of magnetic response. Materials are classified as diamagnetic, paramagnetic, ferromagnetic, ferrimagnetic, ferrite, and superparamagnetic, with examples including ferrous sulfide and nickel oxide. Magnetization and permeability describe bulk response, while hysteresis and the silicon-steel hysteresis loop capture history-dependent behavior. Magnetic boundary conditions govern field transitions between materials. Magnetic circuits introduce magnetomotive force and reluctance as a structured way to reason about flux. Flux linkage leads to self-inductance and mutual inductance, while vector potential provides another route to inductance calculation. Potential energy and forces on magnetic materials connect field storage to mechanical effects. This node should follow steady magnetic fields because inductance and magnetic circuits require magnetic flux, field intensity, and material constitutive behavior, and it should precede time-varying induction because changing flux linkage produces electromotive force.

## Core Ideas

- Electron spin, electron orbit, nuclear spin, and magnetic moments contribute to magnetic response.
- Magnetic materials are classified by the character of their magnetization response.
- Magnetization and permeability connect applied magnetic fields to material response.
- Hysteresis records history-dependent magnetization, including a loop indexed for silicon steel.
- Magnetic boundary conditions constrain fields at material interfaces.
- Magnetomotive force and reluctance form the basis of magnetic-circuit analysis.
- Flux linkage defines self-inductance and supports mutual-inductance calculations.
- Magnetic-field energy produces potential energy and forces on magnetic materials.
