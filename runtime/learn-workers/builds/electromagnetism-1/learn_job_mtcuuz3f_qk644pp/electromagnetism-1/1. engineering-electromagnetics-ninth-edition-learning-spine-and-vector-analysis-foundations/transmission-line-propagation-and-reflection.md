# 1.374 Transmission-Line Propagation and Reflection

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 599, Index entries: Forward-z traveling voltage wave, Initially charged lines, Input impedance, Page 600, Index entries: Net series impedance, Net shunt admittance, Phasor, Page 601, Index entries: Propagation constant, Quarter-wave matching, Reflection coefficients, Page 602, Index entries: Smith chart, Standing wave, Page 603, Index entries: Transient analysis, Transmission line fields and primary constants, Transmission lines, VSWR

Transmission-line theory treats voltage and current as waves distributed along conductors rather than as instantaneous lumped-circuit quantities. The source begins with a physical description of propagation, a lumped-element line model, transmission-line equations, and lossless solutions. Sinusoidal steady-state analysis introduces complex waves, phasor voltage, propagation constant, phase constant, wavelength, velocity, and characteristic behavior. Low-loss approximations and Heaviside's condition organize practical line losses, while instantaneous and time-averaged power support decibel-based loss characterization. Discontinuities create reflected waves, described by reflection and transmission coefficients, standing waves, voltage standing-wave ratio, and load and input impedances. Finite lines, quarter-wave matching, normalized impedances and admittances, and the Smith chart provide reusable impedance-transformation procedures. Transient analysis tracks pulses with voltage and current reflection diagrams and includes initially charged lines and pulse-forming networks. A later treatment derives primary constants from electromagnetic fields for parallel-plate, coaxial, two-wire, and microstrip geometries across frequency regimes.

## Core Ideas

- Distributed line models replace a single lumped connection with position-dependent voltage and current.
- Telegraphist's equations govern line voltage and current propagation.
- Lossless and low-loss solutions introduce propagation and phase constants.
- Load discontinuities generate reflected and transmitted waves.
- Standing-wave ratio measures the strength of line mismatch.
- Input impedance depends on load impedance, line length, and wave impedance.
- Quarter-wave sections and Smith-chart methods support impedance matching.
- Reflection diagrams track transient voltage and current pulses through repeated discontinuity interactions.
- Primary line constants depend on field distributions, geometry, material properties, and frequency.
