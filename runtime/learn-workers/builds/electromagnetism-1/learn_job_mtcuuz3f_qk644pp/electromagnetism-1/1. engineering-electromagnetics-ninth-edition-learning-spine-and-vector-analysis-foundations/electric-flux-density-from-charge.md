# 1.51 Electric Flux Density from Charge

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 61, Page 62, Page 63, Section: 3.1.2 Electric Flux Density

Electric flux density $\mathbf{D}$ measures electric flux crossing unit area normal to the flux direction. It is a vector field with units of $\mathrm{C/m^2}$, directed along the local flux lines. For concentric spherical conductors carrying charges $+Q$ and $-Q$, spherical symmetry distributes $Q$ coulombs of flux uniformly over every intermediate spherical surface. Since a sphere of radius $r$ has area $4\pi r^2$, the flux density is $Q/(4\pi r^2)$ in the outward radial direction. The same expression remains valid in the limit where the inner sphere becomes a point charge. Unlike electric field intensity, which is introduced through force on a test charge, electric flux density is introduced through the distribution of flux generated by source charge. Its form for the spherical configuration is independent of the dielectric between the conductors.

Electric flux density, measured in coulombs per square meter (sometimes described as "lines per square meter," for each line is due to one coulomb), is given the letter **D**, which was originally chosen because of the alternate names of *displacement flux density* or *displacement density*. Electric flux density is more descriptive, however, and we will use the term consistently.

The electric flux density **D** is a vector field and is a member of the "flux density" class of vector fields, as opposed to the "force fields" class, which includes the electric field intensity **E**. The direction of **D** at a point is the direction of the flux lines at that point, and the magnitude is given by the number of flux lines crossing a surface normal to the lines divided by the surface area.

## Core Ideas

- Electric flux density is denoted by $\mathbf{D}$.
- Its units are $\mathrm{C/m^2}$.
- Its direction follows the local electric flux lines.
- Its magnitude is normal-crossing flux divided by area.
- Spherical symmetry spreads total flux over area $4\pi r^2$.
- For a point charge, $\mathbf{D}$ decreases as $1/r^2$.
