# 1.6 Vector Algebra

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

Vector addition follows the parallelogram law and may also be visualized by placing the tail of one vector at the head of another to form a triangle. Addition is commutative, $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{A}$, and associative,
$$
\mathbf{A}+(\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{C})=(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})+\mathbf{C}
$$
 Coplanar vectors can be added by resolving them into horizontal and vertical components, and the same method extends to three dimensions using three components. Subtraction is converted into addition by reversing the second vector: $\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A}+(-\mathbf{B})$. Multiplying a vector by a positive scalar changes its magnitude without changing its direction, while multiplication by a negative scalar also reverses its direction. Division by a scalar means multiplication by its reciprocal. Two vectors are equal when their difference is zero. For vector fields, addition and subtraction are performed between vectors defined at the same spatial point.

Our work will mainly concern scalar and vector $\textit{fields}$. A field (scalar or vector) may be defined mathematically as some function that connects an arbitrary origin to a general point in space. We usually associate some physical effect with a field, such as the force on a compass needle in the earth's magnetic field, or the movement of smoke particles in the field defined by the vector velocity of air in some region of space. Note that the field concept invariably is related to a region. Some quantity is defined at every point in a region. Both $\textit{scalar fields}$ and $\textit{vector fields}$ exist. The temperature and the density at any point in the earth are examples of scalar fields. The gravitational and magnetic fields of the earth, the voltage gradient in a cable, and the temperature gradient in a soldering-iron tip are examples of vector fields. #### 1.2.2 Multiplication and Division

Vectors may be multiplied by scalars. The magnitude of the vector changes, but its direction does not when the scalar is positive, although it reverses direction when multiplied by a negative scalar. ## Core Ideas

- Vector addition follows the parallelogram or head-to-tail construction.
- Vector addition is commutative and associative.
- Components along corresponding directions are added separately.
- Vector subtraction reverses the second vector before addition.
- Positive scalar multiplication preserves direction.
- Negative scalar multiplication reverses direction.
- Vector fields are combined point by point.
