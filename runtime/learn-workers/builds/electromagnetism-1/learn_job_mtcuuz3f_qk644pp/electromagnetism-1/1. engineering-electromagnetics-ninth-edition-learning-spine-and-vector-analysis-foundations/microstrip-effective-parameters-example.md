# 1.267 Microstrip Effective Parameters Example

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 478, Problem D13.4

The opening exercise records the effective propagation parameters of a microstrip line fabricated on a lithium niobate substrate. The substrate has relative permittivity $\epsilon_r=4.8$ and thickness $1\ \text{mm}$, while the top conductor is $2\ \text{mm}$ wide. Because a microstrip field occupies both the dielectric and the surrounding region, its effective relative permittivity is lower than the substrate value. The stated result is $\epsilon_{r,\mathrm{eff}}=3.6$. The corresponding characteristic impedance is $Z_0=47\ \Omega$, and the phase velocity is $v_p=1.6\times10^8\ \text{m/s}$. This example is useful as a transition from quasi-TEM transmission-line propagation to the waveguide modes developed in the rest of the chunk. It provides a concrete contrast: the microstrip is characterized using effective-medium transmission-line quantities, whereas waveguide propagation is subsequently described through discrete TE and TM modes, cutoff, and longitudinal phase constants.

## Page-Grounded Details

#### Page 478

D13.4. A microstrip line is fabricated on a lithium niobate substrate ($\epsilon_{r}=4.8$) of 1 mm thickness. If the top conductor is 2 mm wide, find (a) $\epsilon_{r}$, eff; (b) $Z_{0}$; (c) $v_{p}$.

Ans. (a) 3.6; (b) 47 $\Omega$; (c) $1.6\times 10^{8}m/s$
