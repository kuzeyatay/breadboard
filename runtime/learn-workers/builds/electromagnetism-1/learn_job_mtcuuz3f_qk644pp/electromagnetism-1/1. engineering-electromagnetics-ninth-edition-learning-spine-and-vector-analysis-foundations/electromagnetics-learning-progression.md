# 1.1 Electromagnetics Learning Progression

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 4, Page 5, Page 6, Page 7, Page 8, Page 10

The book organizes engineering electromagnetics as a progression from mathematical language to static fields, dynamic fields, propagation, guidance, and radiation. Vector analysis supplies the initial mathematical tools. Electrostatics then develops Coulomb's law, electric field intensity, electric flux density, Gauss's law, divergence, energy, potential, conductors, dielectrics, and capacitance. Magnetostatics follows through the Biot-Savart law, Ampere's circuit law, curl, Stokes' theorem, magnetic flux, magnetic forces, magnetic materials, and inductance. Faraday's law and displacement current then lead to Maxwell's equations. The later chapters apply the unified theory to transmission lines, uniform plane waves, reflection, dispersion, waveguides, optical fiber, electromagnetic radiation, and antennas. The preface explains that the historical and inductive approach presents experimental laws individually before unifying them in Maxwell's equations. Mathematical tools are introduced when required rather than front-loaded as an abstract formal system. This sequence provides the main Learning Spine into which the detailed concepts from Chapter 1 fit.

## Core Ideas

- Vector analysis precedes the development of electromagnetic field laws.
- Electrostatic topics occupy Chapters 2 through 6.
- Steady magnetic fields, magnetic forces, materials, and inductance occupy Chapters 7 and 8.
- Faraday's law and displacement current lead to Maxwell's equations in Chapter 9.
- Transmission lines, plane waves, reflection, dispersion, and guided waves follow the field equations.
- Radiation and antennas complete the progression.
- Experimental laws are introduced separately and later unified through Maxwell's equations.

## Source Anchors

- Chapter 1 is Vector Analysis, followed by Coulomb's Law and Electric Field Intensity in Chapter 2.
- Chapter 9 contains Faraday's Law, Displacement Current, and Maxwell's Equations in point and integral forms.
- Chapters 10 through 14 cover transmission lines, uniform plane waves, reflection and dispersion, guided waves, and antennas.
- The preface states that the text uses an inductive approach consistent with historical development.
- The preface states that mathematical tools after Chapter 1 are introduced on an as-needed basis.
