# 1.365 Vector Analysis and Electromagnetic Coordinate Systems

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 598, Index entries: Dot product, Page 601, Index entries: Point coordinates, Point transformations, Rectangular coordinate system, Page 602, Index entries: Scalars, Spherical coordinate system, Page 603, Index entries: Unit vectors, Vector analysis, Vector components, Pages 605-606, Vector operator reference

Vector analysis supplies the geometric and algebraic language used throughout the electromagnetic theory indexed in this chunk. The source organizes the subject around scalars and vectors, vector addition and subtraction, unit vectors, vector components, dot and cross products, vector fields, coordinate transformations, and visualization processes. Rectangular coordinates locate points as intersections of planes and use a right-handed set of unit vectors. Spherical coordinates introduce point coordinates, differential surfaces and volumes, coordinate transformations, and transformations of vector components. The separate references to cylindrical and spherical formulas on Pages 605 and 606 show why coordinate selection is not merely notational: the mathematical form of divergence, gradient, curl, and Laplacian depends on the geometry of the coordinate system. This node should precede field laws because electric and magnetic fields are vectors whose components, directions, projections, and derivatives must be interpreted consistently. It also supports later choices of symmetry, such as rectangular coordinates for planar structures, cylindrical coordinates for lines and coaxial cables, and spherical coordinates for radial fields.

## Core Ideas

- Scalars and vectors are introduced as distinct mathematical objects used to describe electromagnetic quantities.
- Vector algebra includes addition, subtraction, multiplication, components, and unit-vector representations.
- The dot product is given geometric and operational definitions and is used with unit vectors.
- The cross product is a separate vector-analysis operation indexed with the foundational material.
- Rectangular coordinates use a right-handed coordinate system and identify points by intersecting coordinate planes.
- Spherical coordinates require coordinate-specific unit vectors, differential surfaces, differential volumes, and component transformations.
- Coordinate transformations connect point and vector descriptions between coordinate systems.
