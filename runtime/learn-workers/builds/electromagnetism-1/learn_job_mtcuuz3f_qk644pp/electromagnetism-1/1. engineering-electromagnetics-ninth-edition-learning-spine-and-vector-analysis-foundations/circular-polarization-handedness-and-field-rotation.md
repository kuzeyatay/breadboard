# 1.237 Circular Polarization Handedness and Field Rotation

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 413, Figure 11.7, Page 413, circular-polarization visualization discussion

A circularly polarized uniform plane wave has an electric-field vector of constant magnitude whose direction rotates in the plane transverse to propagation. At a fixed position such as $z=0$, the field angle varies as $\omega t$ and completes one rotation in $2\pi/\omega$. At a fixed time, following the field direction along the propagation axis produces a corkscrew-like spatial pattern. Figure 11.7 connects these two descriptions by showing how translation of the spatial pattern produces temporal rotation at a fixed observation point. Handedness must be interpreted with an explicitly stated convention. In the convention used by the text, counterclockwise temporal rotation when looking toward the source is right circular polarization, even though the corresponding spatial pattern resembles a left-handed screw. Reversing the pitch reverses the handedness. The spiral staircase is only a visualization aid: the actual field remains a uniform plane wave, infinite over every transverse plane.

## Core Ideas

- At $z=0$, the polarization angle varies as $\omega t$.
- One complete temporal rotation takes $T=2\pi/\omega$.
- At fixed time, the transverse electric-field directions form a corkscrew-like pattern along the propagation axis.
- The stated temporal convention classifies counterclockwise rotation, viewed toward the source, as right circular polarization.
- A different screw-based convention can assign the opposite handedness name to the same wave.
- Reversing the corkscrew pitch reverses polarization handedness.
- The corkscrew does not imply a spatially confined wave.
