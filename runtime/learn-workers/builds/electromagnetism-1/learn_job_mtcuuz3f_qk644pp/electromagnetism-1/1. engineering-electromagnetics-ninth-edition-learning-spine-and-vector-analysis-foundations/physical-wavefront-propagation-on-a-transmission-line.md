# 1.160 Physical Wavefront Propagation on a Transmission Line

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 316, Section 10.1: Physical Description of Transmission Line Propagation

Closing a switch that connects a battery of voltage $V_0$ to a lossless transmission line launches a forward voltage wave $V^+=V_0$ and an associated charging current $I^+$. The voltage does not appear everywhere simultaneously. A wavefront moves along the line and separates the already charged, current-carrying region from the uncharged region carrying no current. Both voltage and current are discontinuous across this idealized front. When the front reaches the load, some or all of the wave may reflect depending on the termination. An open circuit reflects all of the wavefront voltage, while a connected resistor generally produces partial reflection. The demonstration provides a physical interpretation of finite propagation speed and establishes that transmission-line voltage and current should be treated as traveling waves rather than instantaneous circuit variables.

## Core Ideas

- Closing the source switch launches voltage and current waves.
- The wavefront separates charged and uncharged sections of the line.
- Voltage and current are discontinuous across the ideal wavefront.
- The wave travels at finite velocity $v$.
- The load condition determines the amount of reflection.
