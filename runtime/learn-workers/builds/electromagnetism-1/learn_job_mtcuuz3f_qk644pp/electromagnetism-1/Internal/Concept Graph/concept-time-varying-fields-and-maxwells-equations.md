## ConceptNode: Time-Varying Fields and Maxwell's Equations

Planning node for [[time-varying-fields-and-maxwells-equations|1.373 Time-Varying Fields and Maxwell's Equations]].

Locations: Page 598, Index entries: Electromotive force, Faraday's law, Frequency domain analysis, Page 599, Index entries: Lenz's law, Motional EMF, Page 600, Index entry: Maxwell's equations, Page 602, Index entries: Retarded potentials, Sinusoidal steady-state conditions, Page 603, Index entry: Time-varying fields

Time-varying electromagnetics unifies electric and magnetic phenomena through induction and field coupling. Faraday's law relates a changing magnetic field to electromotive force and is indexed in both integral and point forms. Lenz's law fixes the induced response direction, while motional electromotive force treats conductors moving through magnetic fields. The source then introduces displacement current and modifies Ampere's law so that time-varying electric fields contribute alongside conduction current. Maxwell's equations are collected in point and integral forms, with Maxwell's first equation already linked to the differential form of Gauss's law. Retarded potentials account for finite propagation delay between sources and fields, replacing an instantaneous static-field description when source distributions vary with time. Frequency-domain and sinusoidal steady-state analysis provide phasor methods for solving these equations. This node is the conceptual bridge from electrostatics and magnetostatics to electromagnetic waves, transmission lines, radiation, and guided-wave fields because the coupled equations generate wave equations and relate field components.

### Key planning details

- Faraday's law connects changing magnetic flux to induced electromotive force.
- Lenz's law determines the opposing direction of induction.
- Motional electromotive force arises when conductors move through magnetic fields.
- Displacement current modifies Ampere's law for time-varying fields.
- Maxwell's equations are presented in both point and integral forms.
- Retarded potentials include source-to-observer propagation delay.
- Frequency-domain and phasor methods represent sinusoidal steady-state fields.
- Maxwell's equations relate field components in waveguides and produce electromagnetic wave equations.
