## ConceptNode: Microscopic Dipoles and Macroscopic Polarization

Planning node for [[microscopic-dipoles-and-macroscopic-polarization|1.351 Microscopic Dipoles and Macroscopic Polarization]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 583, Section: Origins of the Complex Permittivity

A dielectric is modeled as atoms or molecules whose positive and negative bound charges can be separated by an applied electric field. Each separated charge pair forms a microscopic electric dipole. The molecules may be arranged regularly, as in a crystal, or randomly, as in an amorphous material or liquid. They may also possess permanent dipole moments, although those moments normally have random orientations before a field is applied. The macroscopic polarization $\mathbf{P}$ is defined as the vector dipole moment per unit volume. In a differential volume $\Delta v$ containing $N\Delta v$ dipoles, it is the limiting volume density of the sum of the individual moments: $$\mathbf{P}=\lim_{\Delta v\to 0}\frac{1}{\Delta v}\sum_{i=1}^{N\Delta v}\mathbf{p}_i.$$ The moment of dipole $i$ is $$\mathbf{p}_i=Q_i\mathbf{d}_i,$$ where $Q_i$ is the magnitude of the positive bound charge and $\mathbf{d}_i$ points from the negative charge to the positive charge. This microscopic-to-macroscopic construction is the foundation for both resonant polarization and orientational relaxation.

### Key planning details

- An electric field separates positive and negative bound charges against their Coulomb attraction.
- A microscopic dipole moment is $\mathbf{p}_i=Q_i\mathbf{d}_i$.
- $\mathbf{d}_i$ points from the negative bound charge to the positive bound charge.
- $N$ is the number of dipoles per unit volume.
- Macroscopic polarization is the dipole moment per unit volume.
- Permanent dipoles may exist before a field is applied but ordinarily have random orientations.
