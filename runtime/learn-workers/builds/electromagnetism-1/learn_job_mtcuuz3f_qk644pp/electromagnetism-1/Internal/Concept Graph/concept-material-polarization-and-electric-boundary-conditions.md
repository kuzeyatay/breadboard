The dielectric-material entries connect microscopic polarization to macroscopic electric-field behavior. The source distinguishes polar and nonpolar molecules and indexes polarization field, electric susceptibility, relative permittivity, and ferroelectric materials. These quantities support the description of electric displacement and the response of matter to an applied electric field. Homogeneous dielectrics and capacitors containing multiple dielectrics provide device-level applications, while effective dielectric constant and weighted averages of dielectric constants appear in the treatment of microstrip lines. Boundary behavior is separated into tangential electric field and normal electric flux density conditions. These conditions determine how fields connect across material interfaces and are therefore prerequisites for dielectric capacitor analysis, microstrip approximations, plane-wave reflection, and dielectric waveguides. The source also distinguishes perfect dielectric, lossless medium, good dielectric approximation, and lossy-media propagation. This node should bridge electrostatics and wave propagation by showing that permittivity and polarization affect both stored electric energy and the phase, attenuation, impedance, and refraction of electromagnetic waves.

### Key planning details

- Polar and nonpolar molecules provide microscopic models for dielectric response.
- Polarization field and electric susceptibility quantify material polarization.
- Relative permittivity describes dielectric response relative to free space.
- Tangential electric field and normal electric flux density obey distinct boundary conditions.
- Homogeneous and multiple-dielectric capacitors apply these material properties in electrostatics.
- Effective dielectric constant is used for structures such as microstrip lines.
- Perfect, lossless, good-dielectric, and lossy-medium models represent different propagation regimes.
