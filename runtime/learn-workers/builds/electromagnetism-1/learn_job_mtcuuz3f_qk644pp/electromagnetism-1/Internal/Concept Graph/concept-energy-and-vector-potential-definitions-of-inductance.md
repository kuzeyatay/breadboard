## ConceptNode: Energy and Vector-Potential Definitions of Inductance

Planning node for [[energy-and-vector-potential-definitions-of-inductance|1.130 Energy and Vector-Potential Definitions of Inductance]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 279, Page 280, Page 281, Section 8.10.2

Inductance can be derived from magnetic energy as well as flux linkage. For a linear system carrying current $I$, $L=2W_H/I^2$. Substituting the field-energy expression gives $L=I^{-2}\int_{vol}\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{H}\,dv$. With $\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ and the identity $\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{A}\times\mathbf{H})=\mathbf{H}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{A})-\mathbf{A}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{H})$, the integral separates into a surface term and a volume term. If the bounding surface encloses all magnetic energy so that the surface contribution vanishes, and $\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{J}$, then $L=I^{-2}\int_{vol}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{J}\,dv$. Substituting the vector potential generated by the same current produces double-integral formulas showing that inductance depends on current distribution, material permeability, and conductor geometry. For a filamentary current, the expression reduces through Stokes' theorem to $L=\Phi/I$, or $L=N\Phi/I$ for $N$ identical turns. This derivation establishes the equivalence of the energy, vector-potential, and flux-linkage definitions under the stated linear assumptions.

### Key planning details

- The energy definition is $L=2W_H/I^2$.
- The field form is $L=I^{-2}\int\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{H}\,dv$.
- Using $\mathbf{B}=\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ connects inductance to vector potential.
- The vector identity in Equation (54) separates surface and source terms.
- With a vanishing bounding-surface term, $L=I^{-2}\int\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{J}\,dv$.
- The contributing vector potential must be generated by the current whose self-inductance is being calculated.
- The double-integral forms expose dependence on geometry and current distribution.
- Stokes' theorem recovers $L=\Phi/I$ for a single filamentary loop.
