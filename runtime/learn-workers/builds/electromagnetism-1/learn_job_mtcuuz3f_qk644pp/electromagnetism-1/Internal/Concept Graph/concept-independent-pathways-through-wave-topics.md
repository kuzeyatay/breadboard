## ConceptNode: Independent Pathways Through Wave Topics

Planning node for [[independent-pathways-through-wave-topics|1.2 Independent Pathways Through Wave Topics]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 6, Page 7, Page 10

The preface identifies alternative valid routes through the dynamic portion of the book. Chapter 10 is designed as a stand-alone treatment of transmission lines based entirely on circuit theory. It introduces wave behavior through voltages and currents while treating inductance and capacitance as known line parameters, so it does not depend on the preceding field chapters. Field-based transmission-line concepts and parameter calculations are deferred to Chapter 13, where they also support the introduction of waveguiding. Chapters 11 and 12 remain independent of transmission-line theory, allowing a learner to move directly from Maxwell's equations in Chapter 9 to uniform plane waves in Chapter 11. Under that route, wave phenomena are developed from first principles in the context of electromagnetic fields. Chapter 11 may refer to Chapter 10 for additional perspective, but it contains the material needed to learn plane waves without first studying transmission-line waves. Radiation in Chapter 14 instead builds on the retarded potentials introduced in Chapter 9.

### Key planning details

- Chapter 10 can be studied at the beginning or elsewhere in a course.
- Transmission lines are first treated using circuit voltages and currents.
- Chapter 10 assumes inductance and capacitance as known parameters.
- Transmission-line field concepts and parameter computation appear in Chapter 13.
- A learner may proceed directly from Chapter 9 to Chapter 11.
- Plane-wave instruction does not require prior transmission-line theory.
- Antenna radiation builds on the retarded potentials from Chapter 9.

### Source coverage

- The preface calls Chapter 10 stand-alone and states that it can be read in any part of a course.
- Wave phenomena in Chapter 10 are introduced exclusively as voltages and currents.
- The early part of Chapter 13 introduces transmission-line fields and primary constants.
- The preface explicitly permits progression from Chapter 9 directly to Chapter 11.
- The antennas chapter builds on the retarded-potential discussion in Chapter 9.
