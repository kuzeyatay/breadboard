## ConceptNode: Classification of Magnetic Materials

Planning node for [[classification-of-magnetic-materials|1.118 Classification of Magnetic Materials]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 261, Section preceding 8.6, Table 8.1

Magnetic materials are classified by the relationship between orbital and spin magnetic moments, the internal magnetic flux density produced in response to an applied field, and the organization of atomic moments. Diamagnetic materials have cancelling orbital and spin moments and produce an internal field slightly below the applied field. Paramagnetic materials have a small net atomic moment and produce an internal field slightly above the applied field. Ferromagnetic materials are dominated by spin moments, form magnetic domains, and can produce an internal field much larger than the applied field. Antiferromagnetic materials have strong adjacent moments that oppose one another with nearly equal magnitudes, so their net field response is small. Ferrimagnetic materials also have antiparallel adjacent moments, but the moments are unequal, leaving a substantial net response. Superparamagnetic materials consist of ferromagnetic particles separated by a nonferromagnetic matrix. The matrix prevents domain walls from passing between particles. Table 8.1 provides the comparative framework, while the accompanying text identifies material examples, temperature limitations, electrical conductivity differences, and engineering applications.

### Key planning details

- Diamagnetic materials satisfy approximately $m_{orb}+m_{spin}=0$ and have $B_{int}<B_{appl}$.
- Paramagnetic materials have a small net moment and $B_{int}>B_{appl}$.
- Ferromagnetic materials satisfy $|m_{spin}|\gg|m_{orb}|$ and organize into domains.
- Antiferromagnetic materials have approximately equal adjacent moments aligned in opposite directions.
- Ferrimagnetic materials have unequal adjacent moments aligned in opposite directions.
- Ferrites have low conductivity and therefore reduced eddy-current losses under alternating fields.
- Superparamagnetic materials contain isolated ferromagnetic particles in a nonferromagnetic matrix.
- Antiferromagnetism is generally restricted to relatively low temperatures, while ferrimagnetism disappears above the Curie temperature.

- Ferrites have conductivity several orders of magnitude below that of semiconductors, reducing induced eddy currents and ohmic core losses at higher frequencies.
