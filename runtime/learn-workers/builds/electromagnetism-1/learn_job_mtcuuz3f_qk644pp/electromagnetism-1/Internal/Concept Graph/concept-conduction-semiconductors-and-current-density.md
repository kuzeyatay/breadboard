The conduction sequence links microscopic charge transport to circuit-scale resistance and electromagnetic source terms. Metallic conductors are treated through conductivity, Ohm's law, material influence on current, resistance, and resistivity. Drift velocity and mobility describe carrier motion, while uniform and free current densities describe current distributed through a material. The source also indexes outward-flowing current and Kirchhoff's current law, connecting distributed current to circuit conservation. Semiconductor entries include intrinsic material, doping, donor materials, valence bands, forbidden-band energy gaps, and P-N junction capacitance. These concepts distinguish conductor, insulator, and semiconductor behavior without reducing them to a single resistance model. At high frequencies, current crowding produces skin effect, skin-effect resistance, and loss. The Hall effect and Hall voltage connect moving charge in a magnetic field to a transverse electrical response. This node should support the later appearance of conduction current in Ampere's law, displacement current in Maxwell's correction, and complex propagation constants in conducting media.

### Key planning details

- Ohm's law relates electric-field-driven conduction to current in metallic conductors.
- Conductivity and resistivity are reciprocal material descriptions of conduction behavior.
- Drift velocity and mobility describe microscopic carrier transport.
- Current density represents current distributed through an area or volume.
- Intrinsic semiconductors are modified through doping and donor materials.
- Valence bands and forbidden-band energy gaps distinguish material conduction regimes.
- The Hall effect produces a Hall voltage when moving charge interacts with a magnetic field.
- Skin effect increases the importance of surface-concentrated current and frequency-dependent resistance.
