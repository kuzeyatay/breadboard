## ConceptNode: Steady Magnetic Fields and Magnetic Forces

Planning node for [[steady-magnetic-fields-and-magnetic-forces|1.371 Steady Magnetic Fields and Magnetic Forces]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 598, Index entries: Force, Filament current, Page 599, Index entries: Integral form of Biot-Savart law, Magnetic field, Page 600, Index entries: Lorentz force equation, Magnetic flux, Page 602, Index entries: Steady magnetic field, Stokes' theorem, Page 603, Index entry: Uniform magnetic flux density, Page 605, Section: Curl

The steady-magnetic-field sequence begins with currents as sources of magnetic field. The Biot-Savart law gives the field of a filamentary conductor or finite current element, while Ampere's circuital law provides an efficient route for symmetric current distributions. Indexed applications include the magnetic field within a coaxial cable, around current filaments, from surface current, and inside solenoids and toroids. Curl and Stokes' theorem connect circulation around a closed contour with local field rotation. Magnetic flux density and magnetic field intensity are then used in force calculations. The Lorentz force equation gives the force on a moving charge, and the source extends this to differential current elements, forces between current elements, and force and torque on closed circuits. Uniform magnetic flux density, magnetic dipole moment, and kinetic energy appear in this force framework. Scalar and vector magnetic potentials provide alternative field representations, while the vector potential also supports inductance calculations. This node establishes the magnetostatic foundation needed before electromagnetic induction and Maxwell's time-varying equations.

### Key planning details

- The Biot-Savart law calculates magnetic fields from current elements.
- Ampere's circuital law exploits symmetry in steady-current configurations.
- Indexed field applications include coaxial cables, current filaments, surface currents, solenoids, and toroids.
- Curl and Stokes' theorem connect local and integral descriptions of magnetic circulation.
- The Lorentz force equation gives the magnetic force on a moving charge.
- Current elements and closed circuits experience magnetic force and torque.
- Magnetic scalar and vector potentials provide alternative representations of steady fields.
- Magnetic flux density and field intensity are related but distinct field quantities.
