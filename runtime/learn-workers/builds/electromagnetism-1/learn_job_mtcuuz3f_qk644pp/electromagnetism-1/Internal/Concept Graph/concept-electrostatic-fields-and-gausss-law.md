## ConceptNode: Electrostatic Fields and Gauss's Law

The electrostatic portion of the index begins with electric charge, Coulomb's experimental law, permittivity of free space, and the vector force and electric field of a point charge. Fields from charges at general locations are combined by vector addition, then extended to continuous line, sheet, and volume charge distributions. Symmetry is central to efficient field calculation. The source specifically indexes symmetric point-charge, infinite-line-charge, sheet-charge, and coaxial-cable problems. Gauss's law relates electric flux through a closed Gaussian surface to charge distribution and is developed in integral and differential forms. The progression from a closed surface integral to a differential-sized Gaussian surface, divergence, and Maxwell's first equation connects global flux accounting to the local relation between electric flux density and charge. Electric displacement $\mathbf{D}$ and electric flux density provide the field quantities used in this law. This node should establish when direct superposition is appropriate and when a Gaussian surface adapted to the symmetry reduces the field calculation.

### Key planning details

- Continuous distributions are represented by line, sheet, and volume charge densities.
- Electric flux density and electric displacement are central quantities in Gauss's law.
- A Gaussian surface is selected to exploit charge-distribution symmetry.
- Indexed applications include point charges, infinite line charges, and coaxial cables.
- The differential form of Gauss's law is connected to divergence and Maxwell's first equation.
