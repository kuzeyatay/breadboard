## ConceptNode: Permanent-Dipole Orientation

Planning node for [[permanent-dipole-orientation|1.361 Permanent-Dipole Orientation]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 588, Figure E.3 and permanent-dipole discussion

Some molecules possess permanent electric dipole moments rather than acquiring all of their dipole moment through field-induced charge displacement. In the absence of an applied field, thermal motion leaves these molecular dipoles randomly oriented, so their vector sum and the net macroscopic polarization are approximately zero. When an electric field is applied, it exerts torques that favor alignment of the moments with the field. Even though only a small fraction of the dipoles typically achieves alignment, the resulting statistical bias is enough to produce a measurable polarization and change the material properties. This mechanism requires molecules that can rotate or reorient, so it is most characteristic of liquids and gases. Figure E.3 contrasts a random ensemble with a field-biased ensemble. The aligned drawing is deliberately exaggerated and should not be interpreted as complete molecular ordering. As with induced polarization, the net orientational polarization is represented through a susceptibility relating $\mathbf{P}$ to $\mathbf{E}$.

### Key planning details

- Polar molecules possess dipole moments before an external field is applied.
- Thermal motion produces random dipole orientations without an applied field.
- Random orientations yield little or no net macroscopic polarization.
- An electric field biases dipoles toward alignment with the field.
- Only a small percentage of dipoles typically align.
- Rotational freedom makes the mechanism most relevant to liquids and gases.
