## ConceptNode: Magnetic Field Intensity as a Source-to-Detector Model

Planning node for [[magnetic-field-intensity-source-detector-model|1.101 Magnetic Field Intensity as a Source-to-Detector Model]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 194, Section: The Steady Magnetic Field, Section 7.1: Biot-Savart Law

A field is a convenient intermediate model that divides an interaction problem into two parts: sources establish a field, and detector charges or currents experience measurable forces associated with that field. The field itself is not measured independently of its effect on charged detection equipment. In this chapter, the source of interest is primarily a direct current under steady, free-space conditions. Permanent magnets are set aside, and magnetic fields produced by time-varying electric fields are postponed to later treatment. Material effects and the forces exerted by magnetic fields on other currents are also deferred. This separation defines the immediate task: determine the magnetic field intensity $\mathbf{H}$ produced by a specified steady current distribution. The source-field relationship is more complicated than the electrostatic relationship between charge and electric field, so several magnetostatic laws are initially accepted and derived later in the chapter.

### Key planning details

- Fields divide an interaction into source-to-field and field-to-detector stages.
- Physical measurements ultimately involve forces on charges in detection equipment.
- The immediate source under study is a direct-current distribution in free space.
- Permanent magnets and time-varying electric-field sources are deferred.
- Material media and magnetic forces on other currents are treated later.
- The principal field quantity introduced is magnetic field intensity $\mathbf{H}$.
