The name itself gives the first-principles intuition. *Integrate* means the neuron gathers incoming influence over time instead of reacting only to a single instant. *Leaky* means that this built-up internal state does not remain fixed; if supporting input stops arriving, the stored influence fades. *Fire* means that the neuron emits a spike once enough influence has accumulated. The supplied source does not provide the governing equations of the LIF model, so the safest explanation here is qualitative rather than mathematical. Even at that level, however, the central idea is already clear: timing matters. Inputs arriving close together can combine before earlier influence has faded much, while inputs spread farther apart may have a weaker combined effect because some of the earlier buildup has leaked away.

A simple thought experiment makes that timing intuition easier to feel. Suppose a neuron receives several small input events. If they arrive in a short burst, the neuron can accumulate them quickly enough to produce a spike. If the same events are spread over a longer interval, the earlier buildup may fade before the later events arrive, and the neuron may remain silent. The total amount of input may be similar, but the timing changes the outcome. That is the important neuron-level idea the paper needs before its broader claims about temporal dynamics and spatiotemporal processing can make sense.
