## Threshold Crossing, Spike Emission, and Reset

A Leaky Integrate-and-Fire neuron has already been accumulating incoming signals while leakage gradually reduces their stored effect. Imagine that several inputs arrive close enough together for the membrane potential to keep rising despite that leakage. The neuron now needs a precise rule for deciding when the accumulated input is strong enough to produce an output. That decision is made by the **firing threshold**, a fixed potential level that marks the boundary between continued accumulation and spike emission.

Let $V(t)$ represent the neuron's membrane potential at time $t$, and let $V_{\text{th}}$ represent its firing threshold. As inputs arrive, $V(t)$ changes over time. The neuron checks whether the membrane potential has reached the threshold:
$$
V(t) \geq V_{\text{th}}
$$
Here, $V(t)$ is the accumulated membrane potential at the current time, $V_{\text{th}}$ is the predetermined threshold potential, and the symbol $\geq$ means "greater than or equal to." This condition lets us compute whether the neuron should fire at a particular moment. While the condition is false, the neuron continues integrating input and experiencing leakage. At the moment the condition becomes true, a **threshold crossing** has occurred.

Suppose the threshold is $V_{\text{th}}=1.0$. If the membrane potential rises through the sequence $0.35$, $0.62$, $0.81$, and $0.96$, the neuron remains below threshold at each of those moments. If another input raises the potential to $1.03$, the condition $1.03\geq1.0$ is true. The neuron emits a spike at that time. The amount $1.03$ does not become the spike's numerical size; the threshold crossing triggers a discrete event. The spike records that the firing condition was satisfied and, through its timing, records when it happened.

We can describe that event with a simple spike indicator:
$$
S(t)=
\begin{cases}
1, & V(t)\geq V_{\text{th}},\\
0, & V(t)<V_{\text{th}}.
\end{cases}
$$
The symbol $s(t)$ tells us whether a spike occurs at time $t$. A value of $1$ means that the neuron emits a spike, while a value of $0$ means that it remains silent. The two conditions compare the current membrane potential $V(t)$ with the threshold $V_{\text{th}}$. This rule converts a continuously changing internal quantity-the membrane potential-into a sequence of distinct output events.

Before the trace reaches the threshold, its rising shape represents accumulated input. Any flatter or downward movement reflects periods when new input is weaker and leakage has more influence. The decisive point is where the trace meets the threshold line. That intersection marks the threshold crossing, so the neuron emits a spike there. Immediately afterward, the membrane-potential trace drops to a lower level. That drop represents the **reset** that prepares the neuron to begin another cycle of integration.

A formal reset rule can be written as
$$
V(t^{+})=V_{\text{reset}}\qquad\text{when }V(t)\geq V_{\text{th}}
$$
In this expression, $t$ is the firing time, $t^{+}$ means the moment immediately after firing, $V(t^{+})$ is the membrane potential after the spike, and $V_{\text{reset}}$ is the value to which the potential is reset. The formula lets us determine the neuron's state immediately after a threshold crossing. The exact reset value is a model parameter, just as the threshold is a model parameter.

Consider the earlier numerical example. The membrane potential reaches $1.03$, crosses the threshold of $1.0$, and produces $s(t)=1$. The reset rule then replaces that elevated potential with $V_{\text{reset}}$. Future inputs accumulate from this reset state. The neuron therefore responds to a new collection of incoming signals rather than carrying the entire pre-spike accumulation indefinitely.

Some LIF simulations also include a short **refractory period** after firing. During this interval, the neuron temporarily pauses its ordinary firing cycle. A representative parameter setting uses a threshold of $V_{\text{th}}=1.0$ and a refractory period of $5$ milliseconds. The reset establishes the post-spike membrane potential, while the refractory period establishes a short post-spike interval before ordinary responsiveness resumes. Together, they separate one firing event from the next.

**Question.** If the membrane potential touches the threshold exactly, does the neuron emit a spike?

**Answer.** Yes. The firing condition uses $V(t)\geq V_{\text{th}}$, so equality satisfies the rule. With a threshold of $1.0$, membrane potentials of $1.0$ and $1.03$ both trigger spike emission. A potential of $0.99$ remains below threshold and produces no spike.

A common beginner confusion comes from picturing the spike as the highest point of the membrane-potential curve. The clearer picture separates the neuron's internal state from its output event. The membrane potential rises toward the threshold as input accumulates. Reaching the threshold triggers a spike, represented by $s(t)=1$. The reset then moves the internal potential to $V_{\text{reset}}$. The spike and reset belong to the same firing event, but they describe different parts of it: the spike is the emitted signal, and the reset is the neuron's new internal state.

**Question.** Why reset the membrane potential after every spike?

**Answer.** Resetting gives each firing cycle a defined starting point. After a spike, incoming signals must build the membrane potential toward the threshold again. In the graph, this appears as the sharp drop after each threshold crossing, followed by another period of accumulation. Without that state change, the potential could remain at the firing boundary and repeatedly satisfy the threshold condition without a fresh buildup of input.

The complete mental model is a repeating loop. Incoming signals raise the membrane potential, leakage reduces stored potential over time, and the firing threshold tests whether enough influence has accumulated. A threshold crossing turns that internal accumulation into a precisely timed spike. Reset then returns the neuron to a lower membrane-potential state, allowing the same integrate, leak, compare, fire, and reset process to begin again.
