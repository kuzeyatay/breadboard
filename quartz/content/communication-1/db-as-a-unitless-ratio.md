## dB as a Unitless Ratio

Source: [[988934-english-1|Introduction to Decibels, dBm, and dBW in Engineering]]

Locations: Page 2, Page 3, Page 4

A central conceptual point in the lecture is that plain dB is unitless and expresses only a ratio, not an absolute power. The lecture repeatedly warns students not to interpret dB as a physical power quantity. Instead, dB tells you how many times larger or smaller one value is relative to another on a logarithmic scale. This is useful because multiplicative changes become additive in dB, which simplifies engineering calculations. The lecture explicitly says that dB cannot be converted to dBm, because dBm refers to an actual power relative to a reference level while dB does not. This distinction is described as one of the recurring causes of exam mistakes. The durable concept is that students must first decide whether a quantity is a pure ratio or an absolute level before applying any logarithmic unit or conversion.

### Key points

- Plain dB is unitless.
- dB expresses a ratio or multiplication factor.
- It does not directly represent an absolute power.
- dB simplifies multiplicative calculations by turning them into additions.
- dB cannot be converted directly into dBm.
