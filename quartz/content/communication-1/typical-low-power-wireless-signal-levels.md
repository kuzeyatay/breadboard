## Typical Low-Power Wireless Signal Levels

Source: [[988934-english-1|Introduction to Decibels, dBm, and dBW in Engineering]]

Locations: Page 3

The lecture uses real-world wireless systems to illustrate how dBm values appear in practice. Received signals for technologies such as Wi‑Fi and 5G are described as very low powers, often in the negative dBm range. A specific example given is around -70 dBm, which the lecturer calls a very typical value. This example reinforces that negative dBm does not mean invalid or impossible power; it simply means a power lower than the 1 mW reference used by dBm. The source also connects these low power levels to broader communication-system topics such as error correction and modulation, suggesting that the consequences of weak signals will be discussed later in the course. As a study note, this topic is useful because it ties the abstract logarithmic unit dBm to a familiar engineering context: everyday wireless networks operate with surprisingly small received powers.

### Key points

- Wireless signal powers are often much less than 1 mW.
- Negative dBm values are common in practice.
- A value around -70 dBm is described as typical for wireless reception.
- Mobile phone and Wi‑Fi applications can display such power levels.
- Low power levels matter for later topics such as error correction and modulation.
