## Engineering Use of Decibels for Describing Gain

Source: [[988934-english-1|Introduction to Decibels, dBm, and dBW in Engineering]]

Locations: Page 1, Page 2

The lecture introduces decibels as a standard engineering way to describe changes and relationships between values, especially gain. A typical example is amplifier gain: instead of expressing output-to-input power as a raw multiplication factor, engineers frequently express it in dB. This representation is useful in engineering systems because many relevant component properties, including amplifier gain and antenna gain, are stated this way. The lecture emphasizes that students will use dB repeatedly throughout the course and in exam questions, so understanding the meaning of dB as a ratio is foundational. In this framing, decibels are not presented as a separate physical quantity but as a logarithmic way of expressing how much larger or smaller one value is relative to another. This sets up later distinctions between plain dB, dBm, dBW, and voltage-based decibel forms.

### Key points

- Engineers use decibels to describe relationships between values.
- Amplifier gain is a standard example expressed in dB.
- Antenna gain can also be expressed in dB.
- dB notation will be used often in the course and in exams.
- dB expresses a ratio rather than a standalone physical unit.
