## Physical Channel Learning Objectives

Source: [[communications-1-coursereader|Communications 1 Course Reader - Sampling, PAM, PCM, and Noise]]

Locations: Page 132, Section: 11.1 Learning objectives

The learning objectives define the knowledge scope of Chapter 11 and provide a concise map of the durable competencies expected from the material. The chapter aims to distinguish wired and wireless channels, quantify losses in radio and optical media, and understand wave-guiding mechanisms in fibers. It also includes line-of-sight obstruction via knife-edge models and the effect of fiber mode dispersion on achievable data rates. These objectives matter because they tie mathematical models to engineering tasks: computing received power in RF propagation, estimating attenuation in optical links, differentiating single-mode and multi-mode fiber behavior, and predicting how dispersion constrains communication speed. Unlike a general overview, the objectives frame exactly what procedures students should be able to perform and what concepts they should be able to explain. They also reveal the chapter's dual focus: RF wireless channels used in WiFi and mobile systems, and optical fiber channels used in backbone and increasingly local high-capacity networks.

11 The Physical channel
11.1 Learning objectives
Students completing this chapter should have learned:
1. Can name at least 3 differences between a wired and wireless channel.
2. Can calculate the propagation losses of a wireless signal at various distances with or
without the presence of a knife-edge obstacle.
3. Can calculate the drop in power in an optical fiber based on the wavelength and
attenuation parameters of the fiber.
4. Understand the concept of light guiding in an optical fiber and the difference between
single and multi-mode fibers.
5. Calculate the impact of mode dispersion in a multi-mode fiber on eventual maximum
data transmission speeds.

### Key points

- Students should name at least three differences between wired and wireless channels.
- Students should calculate wireless propagation losses at various distances.
- Knife-edge obstacle effects are included in propagation-loss calculations.
- Students should calculate optical fiber power drop from wavelength and attenuation parameters.
- The chapter expects understanding of light guiding in optical fiber.
- A distinction between single-mode and multi-mode fibers is required.
- Mode dispersion in multi-mode fiber must be linked to maximum data transmission speed.
