## dBW as Absolute Power Relative to 1 Watt

Source: [[988934-english-1|Introduction to Decibels, dBm, and dBW in Engineering]]

Locations: Page 4

The lecture clarifies that dBW is another absolute logarithmic power unit, but unlike dBm its reference is 1 watt rather than 1 milliwatt. This makes dBW directly convertible to dBm because both represent actual powers on logarithmic scales with fixed references. The worked example in the source is that 1 watt equals 0 dBW. Expressing the same 1 watt relative to 1 milliwatt gives 30 dBm, because 1 watt is 1000 milliwatts and $$10\log_{10}(1000)=30.$$ The lecture identifies this as a likely source of student confusion: the fact that 0 dBW equals 30 dBm does not mean that plain dB can be converted to dBm by adding 30. The durable concept is that conversions are valid only between quantities that are both absolute powers and whose references are known.

### Key points

- dBW is an absolute power unit referenced to 1 watt.
- 1 watt equals 0 dBW.
- The same 1 watt equals 30 dBm.
- The conversion works because both dBW and dBm are absolute power units.
- This relationship does not justify converting plain dB to dBm.
