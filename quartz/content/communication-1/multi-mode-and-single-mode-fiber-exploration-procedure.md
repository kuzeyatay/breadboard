## Multi-Mode and Single-Mode Fiber Exploration Procedure

Source: [[communications-1-coursereader|Communications 1 Course Reader - Sampling, PAM, PCM, and Noise]]

Locations: Page 143, Section: Minilab exercise 12.1

The mini-lab turns fiber propagation into an experimental procedure for understanding ray angle, total internal reflection, and mode support. In the default multimode configuration, the learner increases laser angle and observes how propagation time changes. The lab asks the learner to identify the angle beyond which total internal reflection no longer occurs and to calculate the values seen in the simulator. This creates a direct link between geometry, refractive index, and guided propagation. The exercise then shifts to the 'Modes and fiber settings' tab to explore single-mode conditions by changing wavelength and core diameter and plotting the modes equation. The learner is asked to find an angle that satisfies the propagation condition and then verify on the fiber-optic channel tab whether the ray actually propagates in a single-mode fiber. The procedure teaches that single-mode guidance is more restrictive than multimode guidance because the allowed angle set is narrow and dependent on fiber parameters.

### Key points

- Increasing laser angle in the default setup changes ray propagation time.
- Beyond a certain angle, total internal reflection no longer occurs.
- The mini-lab asks learners to calculate the observed values rather than just inspect them.
- Single-mode exploration requires changing wavelength and core diameter.
- The plotted modes equation helps identify allowed propagation angles.
- A candidate angle must be validated on the fiber-optic channel tab.
- The exercise emphasizes the difficulty of finding a valid angle for single-mode propagation.
