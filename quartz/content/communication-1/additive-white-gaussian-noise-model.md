## Additive White Gaussian Noise Model

Source: [[communications-1-coursereader|Communications 1 Course Reader - Sampling, PAM, PCM, and Noise]]

Locations: Page 54, Page 55

Additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) is introduced as the standard channel-noise model used to represent the combined effect of many random disturbances such as thermal noise. The term 'additive' means the noise is simply added to the transmitted signal so that the receiver observes signal plus noise. 'White' refers to the spectral property that the noise has constant power spectral density across frequency, denoted by $N_0/2$ in the two-sided representation. 'Gaussian' refers to the amplitude distribution of the noise samples, which follow a normal distribution with mean $\mu_x = 0$. The section then connects the spectral and statistical views by stating that the variance of the Gaussian noise equals the total noise power in the receiver bandwidth. With a receiver low-pass filter of bandwidth $B$, the total noise power is $$N = \sigma^2 = \left(\frac{N_0}{2}\right)(2B) = N_0B.$$ This is explained by integrating the constant two-sided spectral density over both positive and negative frequencies admitted by the filter. The AWGN model is therefore both a PSD model in frequency and a Gaussian random variable model in amplitude, making it the natural basis for later bit-error calculations.

### Key points

- AWGN models many random physical noise processes in telecommunications.
- Additive means the noise adds directly to the signal.
- White means the PSD is constant over frequency.
- The two-sided PSD is denoted by $N_0/2$.
- Gaussian means the noise amplitudes follow a normal distribution with zero mean.
- The total noise power in bandwidth $B$ is $N = N_0B$.
- Noise variance equals the total AWGN noise power in the receiver bandwidth.
