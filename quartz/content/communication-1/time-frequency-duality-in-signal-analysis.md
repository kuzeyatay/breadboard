## Time-Frequency Duality in Signal Analysis

Source: [[988929-english-3|Communication 1 Course Introduction and Foundations of Digital Communication]]

Locations: Page 7, Page 8

After presenting the communication chain, the lecture turns to a conceptual tool used throughout signal analysis: the relation between time and frequency. Communication signals are treated as time-dependent, but one of the strongest engineering tools is to move between time-domain and frequency-domain descriptions. The motivation is practical: to understand what frequencies are contained in a signal, because those frequencies matter for analysis and later for communication system design. This dual viewpoint sets up the need for signal decomposition methods such as Fourier series. The lecture frames frequency analysis not as an abstract mathematics topic, but as a core engineering method for understanding and manipulating communication signals.

### Key points

- Communication is described as being carried out in time-dependent signals.
- Frequency is presented as one of the strongest engineering tools.
- Engineers often switch between time-domain and frequency-domain views.
- The purpose is to identify which frequencies are present in a signal.
- This perspective motivates later use of Fourier series.
