## Quiz-Based Retrieval Practice in the Course

Source: [[988929-english-3|Communication 1 Course Introduction and Foundations of Digital Communication]]

Locations: Page 1, Page 2, Page 4, Page 8

A central teaching claim in the lecture is that repeated testing is one of the most effective ways to learn. The instructors cite studies and present retrieval practice as superior to passive review. They also distinguish genuine self-testing from sending quiz questions to an LLM and merely reading an answer, arguing that reading generated answers does not create transferable understanding. The course redesign operationalizes this principle through online quizzes, module-linked preparation tasks, and short entry quizzes at the start of lectures. These quizzes are used both for student learning and for instructor diagnosis of misconceptions. The approach therefore treats quizzing not as isolated assessment, but as a feedback loop between students and teachers.

### Key points

- The lecture claims that the best way to learn is to test knowledge repeatedly.
- Using an LLM to answer quiz questions is presented as ineffective for building understanding.
- Quizzes are intended to prepare students for later application, especially under exam conditions.
- A short Canvas quiz at the beginning of class is used to reveal gaps from the previous lecture.
- The more students use quizzes, the better instructors can adapt lectures to student needs.
- Quizzing is used as both a learning tool and a diagnostic tool.
