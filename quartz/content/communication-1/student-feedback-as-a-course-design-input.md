## Student Feedback as a Course Design Input

Source: [[988929-english-3|Communication 1 Course Introduction and Foundations of Digital Communication]]

Locations: Page 1, Page 2

The lecture begins by emphasizing that course evaluation and student feedback are treated as operational input for improving the course. The instructors explain that low response rates create a distorted picture because strongly positive and strongly negative students respond more often than average students. Feedback is gathered both through formal surveys and through the student body, which holds regular meetings and passes information directly to instructors. Concrete teaching changes are attributed to prior feedback, such as adding a topic list at the beginning of each lecture to improve structure and changing how instruction moments are used. The instructors frame feedback as an iterative mechanism that helps them adapt lectures, quizzes, and interaction style to better support learning. This creates a durable idea about educational design: feedback is useful when it is specific enough to influence course organization, learning activities, and communication structure.

### Key points

- Low survey participation makes it hard to know what the average student thinks.
- Responses are often split between students who liked the course and students who disliked it.
- Feedback also comes through the student body during regular quarter meetings.
- A prior complaint was that instruction moments were not effective and felt like wasted time.
- A topic list is now written on the board at the start of lectures to add structure.
- The instructors explicitly state that they use feedback to change course organization.
