## Decibel Power Ratios

Source: [[997203-english|Decibels, Fourier Representation, Digital Sampling, and Nyquist Reconstruction]]

Locations: Page 1

The lecture recap establishes several durable facts about decibels as logarithmic ratios. For power quantities, doubling the power corresponds to an increase of approximately $3\,\mathrm{dB}$. A zero-decibel difference between two power levels means the two powers are equal, because a logarithmic ratio of zero corresponds to a ratio of one. Negative dB values are not impossible and do not mean zero power; they mean the measured quantity is smaller than its reference. This implies the underlying linear ratio is a fraction, such as $0.1$ of the reference. The lecture stresses the importance of identifying whether the quantity is a power or a field quantity, because voltage ratios use a different decibel conversion rule than power ratios. These facts are used as foundational fluency for later communication-system calculations, including signal strength, spectral components, and power references.

### Key points

- Doubling a power quantity corresponds to approximately $+3\,\mathrm{dB}$.
- A difference of $0\,\mathrm{dB}$ between two power levels means the powers are equal.
- Negative dB values mean the quantity is smaller than the reference.
- Negative dB values correspond to fractional linear ratios, not zero power.
- Power ratios and voltage ratios must not be treated with the same dB conversion rule.
- The lecture uses dB fluency as a prerequisite for later communication-system topics.
