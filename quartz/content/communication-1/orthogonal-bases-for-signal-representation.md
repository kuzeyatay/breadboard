## Orthogonal Bases for Signal Representation

Source: [[997203-english|Decibels, Fourier Representation, Digital Sampling, and Nyquist Reconstruction]]

Locations: Page 3, Page 4

The lecture reviews the concept of an orthogonal basis by first using a three-dimensional space analogy. A point in three-dimensional space can be represented using fundamental basis vectors. Orthogonality is defined through products of basis vectors: the product gives a nonzero constant $K$ when comparing a basis vector with itself and zero in all other cases. The lecture then extends this idea from ordinary vectors to functions. Functions can serve as basis functions, allowing a signal to be represented by coefficients along those basis functions. This concept motivates Fourier representation, where sine and cosine functions act as an orthogonal set of basis functions for expressing a time-domain waveform in the frequency domain. The key durable idea is that changing domains does not change the signal itself; it changes the basis used to describe it.

### Key points

- An orthogonal basis allows a point or signal to be represented by fundamental components.
- In the vector analogy, a point in three-dimensional space is represented using fundamental vectors.
- Orthogonality is described by products that are nonzero for matching basis vectors and zero otherwise.
- The lecture states the nonzero result as $K$ when the basis vectors are the same.
- The same idea can be applied to functions rather than only geometric vectors.
- Fourier methods use sine and cosine functions as basis functions for signal representation.
