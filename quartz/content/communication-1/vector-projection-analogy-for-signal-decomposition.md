## Vector Projection Analogy for Signal Decomposition

Source: [[988929-english-3|Communication 1 Course Introduction and Foundations of Digital Communication]]

Locations: Page 7, Page 8, Page 9

To motivate Fourier analysis, the lecture uses a linear algebra analogy. In three-dimensional space, a vector can be represented by its projections onto orthogonal basis vectors along the $x$, $y$, and $z$ axes. If a vector has no component along the $z$ axis, taking a scalar product with the $z$ basis vector yields zero. The lecture extends this idea to signals by asking whether a periodic function can be written as a combination of orthogonal basis functions. The durable lesson is that signal decomposition is conceptually the same as vector decomposition: determine how much of each basis direction, or basis function, is present in the object being analyzed.

### Key points

- A spatial vector can be decomposed into components along orthogonal axes.
- Scalar multiplication or projection reveals the size of each component.
- A zero projection means the vector has no component in that basis direction.
- The same idea is applied to periodic functions.
- Signals can be viewed as combinations of orthogonal basis functions.
