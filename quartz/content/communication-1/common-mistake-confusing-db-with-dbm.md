## Common Mistake: Confusing dB with dBm

Source: [[988934-english-1|Introduction to Decibels, dBm, and dBW in Engineering]]

Locations: Page 3, Page 4

A repeated warning in the lecture is that students often confuse plain dB with dBm, and this leads to avoidable exam errors. The lecturer strongly states that dB cannot be converted into dBm because the two quantities are fundamentally different: dB is a unitless ratio, while dBm is an absolute power relative to 1 mW. The source mentions that every year students assert incorrect rules such as 'dB plus 30 equals dBm', and the instructor rejects this as wrong when applied to plain dB. The only legitimate conversions are between absolute power units with defined references, such as dBm and dBW. This topic is durable because it captures a common conceptual failure mode in engineering calculations: mixing relative gains with absolute power levels without tracking the reference. The lecture explicitly says points are lost on exams because of this confusion, so students should treat the distinction as essential.

### Key points

- Students frequently confuse dB and dBm.
- dB is a ratio, while dBm is an absolute power referenced to 1 mW.
- Plain dB cannot be converted directly into dBm.
- Rules like 'dB + 30 = dBm' are incorrect when applied to plain dB.
- This confusion causes repeated exam mistakes.
