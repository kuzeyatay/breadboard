# 1.274 TE Mode Fields from Plane-Wave Superposition

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 488, Section 13.3.3 and Equations (45) through (49)

The TE modal field can be constructed by superposing upward and downward plane waves. Their wavevectors are
$$
\mathbf{k}_u=\kappa_m\mathbf{a}_x+\beta_m\mathbf{a}_z,\qquad \mathbf{k}_d=-\kappa_m\mathbf{a}_x+\beta_m\mathbf{a}_z
$$
 Because TE reflection introduces a $\pi$ phase shift, the reflected electric-field phasor enters with a minus sign. Substitution and simplification produce
$$
E_{ys}=E_0'\sin(\kappa_m x)e^{-j\beta_m z}
$$
 In instantaneous form above cutoff,
$$
E_y(x,z,t)=E_0'\sin(\kappa_m x)\cos(\omega t-\beta_m z)
$$
 The sine factor is a fixed transverse interference pattern, while the cosine factor describes propagation along $z$. Thus a mode is not a single oblique ray. It is the stable interference pattern generated by the complete set of reflected constituent waves. The conducting-wall zeros arise naturally because $\sin(\kappa_m x)$ vanishes at $x=0$ and $x=d$ for the allowed values $\kappa_m=m\pi/d$.

$$
E_{ys}=E_{0}e^{-j\mathbf{k}_{u}\cdot\mathbf{r}}-E_{0}e^{-j\mathbf{k}_{d}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\quad{(45)}

## Core Ideas

- The upward and downward waves share the same axial component $\beta_m$.
- Their transverse components are $+\kappa_m$ and $-\kappa_m$.
- The TE reflection phase creates a subtraction between constituent electric fields.
- Superposition produces the transverse factor $\sin(\kappa_m x)$.
- The factor $e^{-j\beta_m z}$ describes forward axial propagation.
- The transverse field profile remains fixed while its phase advances down the guide.
- The sine profile satisfies zero tangential electric field at both conducting plates.

## Source Anchors

- Equation (45) writes the TE field as incident field minus reflected field.
- Equations (46) and (47) define $\mathbf{k}_u$ and $\mathbf{k}_d$.
- Equation (48) reduces the superposition to $E_0'\sin(\kappa_m x)e^{-j\beta_m z}$.
- The instantaneous field following Equation (48) contains $\cos(\omega t-\beta_m z)$.
- The source interprets the mode as an interference pattern with transverse variation and positive-$z$ propagation.
