# 1.8 Differential Elements in Rectangular Coordinates

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 16, Page 17

Small coordinate changes around a general point produce the differential geometric elements used later in line, surface, and volume integrals. Starting at $P(x,y,z)$, increasing the coordinates by $dx$, $dy$, and $dz$ gives a nearby point $P'(x+dx,y+dy,z+dz)$. The original and displaced constant-coordinate planes enclose a rectangular parallelepiped. Its differential volume is
$$
dv=dx\,dy\,dz
$$
 Its three pairs of faces have differential area magnitudes $dx\,dy$, $dy\,dz$, and $dz\,dx$. The differential distance from $P$ to $P'$ is the diagonal, with magnitude
$$
dL=\sqrt{(dx)^2+(dy)^2+(dz)^2}
$$
 Figure 1.2(c) represents this volume element and emphasizes that $dx$, $dy$, and $dz$ are generally independent differentials. These scalar geometric quantities later support vector line elements, oriented surface elements, flux integrals, and volume integrations in electromagnetic field analysis.

## Core Ideas

- A nearby point is $P'(x+dx,y+dy,z+dz)$.
- Six constant-coordinate planes enclose a differential rectangular volume.
- The volume element is $dv=dx\,dy\,dz$.
- Face-area magnitudes are $dx\,dy$, $dy\,dz$, and $dz\,dx$.
- The diagonal distance is $dL=\sqrt{(dx)^2+(dy)^2+(dz)^2}$.
- The coordinate differentials are generally independent.
- These elements prepare for line, surface, and volume integrals.
