# 1.368 Electric Potential, Energy, and Capacitance

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 598, Index entries: Electric field, Electrostatic energy, Energy, Equipotential surface, Page 599, Index entries: Laplace's equations, Line integral, Page 600, Index entry: Multiple dielectrics, Page 601, Index entries: Poisson's equations, Potential difference, Potential field, Potential gradient, Page 602, Index entries: Spherical capacitor, Stored energy, Page 603, Index entries: Two-dimensional problems, Two-wire line, Pages 605-606, Sections: Gradient, Laplacian

Electric potential provides a scalar description of electrostatic fields and connects field calculations to energy and capacitance. The source develops line integrals through their summation interpretation, mechanics, uniform-field case, and worked examples. Potential difference is then related to potential fields produced by a point charge, an ensemble of point charges, and continuous charge distributions. The static electric field is conservative, so it can be recovered as the negative gradient of potential. In compact form, $\mathbf{E}=-\nabla V$, where $V$ is electric potential and $\mathbf{E}$ is electric-field intensity. Equipotential surfaces and the potential gradient provide complementary geometric interpretations. Laplace's and Poisson's equations support boundary-value solutions, including a listed P-N junction capacitance example. The energy sequence covers work expended in moving a point charge, energy stored in a charge distribution, energy stored in an electric field, and field energy in a coaxial cable. Capacitance applications include homogeneous and multiple dielectrics, parallel-plate, spherical, two-wire, coaxial, and two-dimensional field-sketch methods.

## Core Ideas

- A line integral accumulates the electric-field contribution along a path.
- Electrostatic potential is defined for point charges, systems of charges, and continuous distributions.
- A static electric field is conservative.
- The field-potential relation is $\mathbf{E}=-\nabla V$.
- Laplace's and Poisson's equations formulate electrostatic boundary-value problems.
- Electrostatic energy can be expressed for charge distributions or as energy stored in the field.
- Capacitance depends on conductor geometry and dielectric configuration.
- Field sketches can estimate capacitance in two-dimensional problems.
