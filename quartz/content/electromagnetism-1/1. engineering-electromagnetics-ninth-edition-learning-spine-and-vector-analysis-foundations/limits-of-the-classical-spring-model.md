# 1.360 Limits of the Classical Spring Model

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 587, final paragraph, Page 588, opening continuation

The Lorentz spring model can predict the frequency dependence of dielectric constants accurately, especially away from resonance, and it can approximate some absorption behavior. Its limitation is not the oscillator mathematics but its classical assumption about atomic energy. The model allows the bound electron to occupy a continuum of energy states. Real atomic systems instead have discrete, quantized energy levels. Processes involving transitions between these levels, including spontaneous emission, stimulated emission, and absorption, are therefore absent from the classical model. A full treatment of these effects requires quantum mechanics. The source nevertheless notes an important correspondence principle: at very low applied-field amplitudes, quantum-mechanical polarization results often reduce to forms resembling the classical spring model. The classical model is consequently useful as a low-field macroscopic approximation, but it should not be treated as a complete microscopic account of resonant material behavior.

## Core Ideas

- The spring model predicts dielectric behavior particularly well away from resonance.
- It can approximate some absorption properties.
- It incorrectly permits a continuum of electron energy states.
- Actual atomic energy levels are quantized.
- Spontaneous and stimulated absorption and emission require a quantum description.
- Low-field quantum results often reduce to the classical oscillator form.
