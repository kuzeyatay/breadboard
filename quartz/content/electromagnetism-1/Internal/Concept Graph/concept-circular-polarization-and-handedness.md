## ConceptNode: Circular Polarization and Handedness

Planning node for [[circular-polarization-and-handedness|1.236 Circular Polarization and Handedness]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 411, Page 412, Figure 11.6

Circular polarization is the special elliptical-polarization case in which the perpendicular components have equal amplitudes, $E_{x0}=E_{y0}=E_0$, and relative phase $\phi=\pm\pi/2$. The instantaneous field is $\mathbf{E}(z,t)=E_0[\cos(\omega t-\beta z)\mathbf{a}_x\mp\sin(\omega t-\beta z)\mathbf{a}_y]$. At a fixed point, the field magnitude remains constant while its direction rotates, so the vector tip traces a circle. For positive $z$ propagation, choosing $\phi=+\pi/2$ produces clockwise rotation and left circular polarization, while choosing $\phi=-\pi/2$ produces counterclockwise rotation and right circular polarization under the convention used by the source. Figure 11.6 depicts the counterclockwise rotation of the right circularly polarized field described by Equation (98). Handedness is determined by placing the appropriate hand's thumb in the propagation direction and checking whether the curled fingers follow the field's temporal rotation. The instantaneous field angle satisfies $\theta(z,t)=\tan^{-1}(E_y/E_x)=\mp(\omega t-\beta z)$, linking rotation sense directly to phase choice and propagation.

### Key planning details

- Circular polarization requires equal perpendicular-component amplitudes.
- The required relative phase is $\phi=\pm\pi/2$.
- The total electric-field magnitude remains constant.
- The field-vector tip traces a circle at a fixed point.
- For positive $z$ travel, $\phi=+\pi/2$ gives left circular polarization.
- For positive $z$ travel, $\phi=-\pi/2$ gives right circular polarization.
- Handedness combines the field rotation direction with the propagation direction.
- The field angle is $\theta(z,t)=\mp(\omega t-\beta z)$.
