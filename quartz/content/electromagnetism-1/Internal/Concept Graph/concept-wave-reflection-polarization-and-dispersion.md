## ConceptNode: Wave Reflection, Polarization, and Dispersion

Planning node for [[wave-reflection-polarization-and-dispersion|1.376 Wave Reflection, Polarization, and Dispersion]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 599, Index entries: Group delay difference, Group velocity, Page 600, Index entries: Normal incidence, Oblique incidence, Phase velocity, Page 601, Index entries: Plane wave analysis, Pulse broadening, Quarter-wave layers, Page 602, Index entries: Snell's law, Spectral packet, Page 603, Index entry: Uniform plane wave, Page 604, Index entries: Wave polarization, Wave reflection

This node gathers three linked behaviors of plane waves: interaction with interfaces, vector polarization, and frequency-dependent propagation. At normal incidence, the source develops incident, reflected, and transmitted waves, reflection and transmission coefficients, total and partial reflection, and power reflectivity. Multiple interfaces introduce a two-interface problem, wave impedance transformations, half-wave and quarter-wave layers, and matching. At oblique incidence, the plane of incidence separates parallel or p-polarized waves from perpendicular or s-polarized waves. Snell's law, polarization angle, total internal reflection, total transmission, and effective impedances determine the interface response. Polarization is classified as linear, elliptical, circular, left-handed, or right-handed according to the relative amplitudes and phases of transverse field components. Dispersion is organized through refractive index, the $\omega$-$\beta$ diagram, phase velocity, group velocity, group delay difference, spectral packets, and pulse broadening. The transform-limited pulse envelope and temporal-prism entries emphasize how spectral components spread in a dispersive medium.

### Key planning details

- Reflection and transmission coefficients relate incident fields to reflected and transmitted fields.
- Power reflectivity distinguishes power ratios from field-amplitude ratios.
- Quarter-wave and half-wave layers modify reflection through interference and impedance transformation.
- At oblique incidence, p polarization is parallel to the plane of incidence and s polarization is perpendicular to it.
- Snell's law connects propagation directions across an interface.
- Linear, elliptical, and circular polarization arise from different amplitude and phase relations between transverse components.
- Phase velocity tracks a constant phase, while group velocity tracks the motion of a wave packet.
- Frequency-dependent propagation causes dispersion and pulse broadening.
- The $\omega$-$\beta$ diagram represents the relationship between angular frequency and phase constant.
