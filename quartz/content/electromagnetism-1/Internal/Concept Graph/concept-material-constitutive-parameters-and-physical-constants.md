## ConceptNode: Material Constitutive Parameters and Physical Constants

Planning node for [[material-constitutive-parameters-and-physical-constants|1.349 Material Constitutive Parameters and Physical Constants]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 578, Appendix C, Page 579, Table C.1, Page 580, Tables C.2-C.4

The material appendix organizes representative electromagnetic properties into relative permittivity, dielectric loss tangent, conductivity, relative permeability, and physical constants. Table C.1 lists $\epsilon_r'$ and $\epsilon''/\epsilon'$ for insulating and dielectric materials under normal temperature and humidity at very low audio frequencies. Values range widely, from air near 1 to barium titanate at 1200, while loss tangents distinguish low-loss materials such as polystyrene from more lossy materials such as snow. Table C.2 lists room-temperature, zero-frequency conductivities in decreasing order, from silver at $6.17\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}$ and copper at $5.80\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}$ down to highly insulating quartz at $10^{-17}\ \mathrm{S/m}$. Table C.3 shows relative permeabilities close to 1 for diamagnetic and paramagnetic materials and much larger values for ferromagnetic materials, reaching 100,000 for supermalloy in the representative table. The source warns that ferromagnetic values apply only at very low flux densities and that maximum permeability may be an order of magnitude higher. Table C.4 supplies electron charge and mass together with $\epsilon_0$, $\mu_0$, and $c$.

### Key planning details

- Relative permittivity and loss tangent characterize dielectric storage and loss.
- The dielectric values are representative and condition-dependent.
- Conductivity values apply at zero frequency and room temperature.
- Silver and copper are the highest-conductivity metals listed.
- Diamagnetic and paramagnetic relative permeabilities remain close to 1.
- Ferromagnetic permeability depends strongly on flux density.
- Table C.4 collects electron and free-space physical constants.

### Source coverage

- Page 578 states the conditions and limitations for Tables C.1 through C.4.
- Table C.1 lists air at $\epsilon_r'=1.0005$, polyethylene at 2.26 with loss tangent 0.0002, water at 80 with loss tangent 0.04, and barium titanate at 1200.
- Table C.2 lists silver at $6.17\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}$, copper at $5.80\times10^7\ \mathrm{S/m}$, sea water at $5\ \mathrm{S/m}$, and quartz at $10^{-17}\ \mathrm{S/m}$.
- Table C.3 lists bismuth at $\mu_r=0.9999986$, ferrite at 1000, mumetal at 20,000, and supermalloy at 100,000.
- Table C.4 gives $e=1.60217733\times10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}$ and electron mass $9.1093897\times10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg}$.
- Table C.4 gives $\epsilon_0$, $\mu_0$, and $c$.
