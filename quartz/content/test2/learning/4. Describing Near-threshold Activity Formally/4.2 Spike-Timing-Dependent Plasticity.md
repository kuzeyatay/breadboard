## Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity

Once a neuron's near-threshold activity produces a spike, that event can influence another neuron through a synaptic connection. Imagine a presynaptic neuron sending a spike across the connection, followed shortly by a spike from the postsynaptic neuron. The close sequence suggests that the incoming event participated in bringing the receiving neuron to threshold. A learning rule can use this timing relationship to adjust the connection, making future presynaptic spikes more or less influential.

This form of learning is called **spike-timing-dependent plasticity**, or **STDP**. *Plasticity* means that a synaptic connection can change its strength. *Spike-timing-dependent* means that the direction of this change is determined by the order in which the presynaptic and postsynaptic spikes occur. The rule needs information available at a single connection: the two spike times and the connection's current weight. For that reason, STDP is a **local learning rule**.

Consider two neurons connected by a synapse. Let neuron $j$ be the presynaptic neuron, which sends a spike into the connection, and let neuron $i$ be the postsynaptic neuron, which receives that influence. Their spike times are

- $t_j$: the presynaptic spike time,
- $t_i$: the postsynaptic spike time.

The quantity that captures their order is the **pre-post timing difference**
$$
\Delta t = t_j-t_i
$$
This subtraction turns two clock readings into one useful signal. If the presynaptic neuron fires at $t_j=12\text{ ms}$ and the postsynaptic neuron fires at $t_i=15\text{ ms}$, then
$$
\Delta t=12-15=-3\text{ ms}
$$
The negative sign tells us that the presynaptic spike occurred first. If the order is reversed, with $t_j=15\text{ ms}$ and $t_i=12\text{ ms}$, then $\Delta t=3\text{ ms}$, indicating that the postsynaptic spike occurred first.

STDP converts this timing signal into a change in synaptic weight. Let $w_{ij}$ denote the strength of the connection from presynaptic neuron $j$ to postsynaptic neuron $i$. The change applied to that strength is written as $\Delta w_{ij}$. A timing-based update rule is
$$
\Delta w_{ij}=
\begin{cases}
A_{+}w_{ij}(1-w_{ij}), & \text{if } t_j-t_i\leq 0,\\[4pt]
A_{-}w_{ij}(1-w_{ij}), & \text{if } t_j-t_i>0.
\end{cases}
$$
Here, $a_{+}$ is the learning-rate coefficient for strengthening the connection, $a_{-}$ is the signed learning-rate coefficient for weakening it, $w_{ij}$ is the current synaptic weight, and $\Delta w_{ij}$ is the amount added to that weight. For synaptic depression, $a_{-}$ takes a negative value. After the timing pair is observed, the connection can be updated through
$$
W_{ij}^{\text{new}}=w_{ij}^{\text{old}}+\Delta w_{ij}
$$
The formula therefore computes both the direction and size of one weight update. Timing order selects one of the two branches. When $t_j-t_i\leq 0$, the presynaptic spike occurs before or at the same time as the postsynaptic spike, so the $a_{+}$ branch produces **synaptic potentiation**, meaning an increase in connection strength. When $t_j-t_i>0$, the postsynaptic spike occurs before the presynaptic spike, so the negative $a_{-}$ branch produces **synaptic depression**, meaning a decrease in connection strength.

Suppose $w_{ij}=0.4$ and $a_{+}=0.1$. A presynaptic spike at $8\text{ ms}$ followed by a postsynaptic spike at $11\text{ ms}$ gives
$$
T_j-t_i=8-11=-3\text{ ms}
$$
So the potentiation branch applies:
$$
\Delta w_{ij}
=0.1(0.4)(1-0.4)
=0.1(0.4)(0.6)
=0.024
$$
The updated weight is therefore
$$
W_{ij}^{\text{new}}=0.4+0.024=0.424
$$
The connection becomes slightly stronger. Future spikes from neuron $j$ can consequently have greater influence on neuron $i$.

Now reverse the spike order while keeping the same current weight. Let the postsynaptic neuron fire at $8\text{ ms}$ and the presynaptic neuron fire at $11\text{ ms}$. Then $t_j-t_i=3\text{ ms}$, so the depression branch applies. With $a_{-}=-0.08$,
$$
\Delta w_{ij}
=-0.08(0.4)(0.6)
=-0.0192
$$
Giving
$$
W_{ij}^{\text{new}}=0.4-0.0192=0.3808
$$
Reversing the order reverses the direction of learning. The coefficients $a_{+}$ and $a_{-}$ can also give potentiation and depression different update magnitudes.

The factor $w_{ij}(1-w_{ij})$ makes the current connection strength part of the update. For a weight near $0$, the $w_{ij}$ factor is small. For a weight near $1$, the $1-w_{ij}$ factor is small. The largest product occurs around the middle of the range. For example,
$$
0.1(1-0.1)=0.09,\qquad
0.5(1-0.5)=0.25,\qquad
0.9(1-0.9)=0.09
$$
A middle-strength connection therefore changes more per spike pair than a connection near either boundary, assuming the same learning-rate coefficient.

Within this particular rule, the timing difference controls the update through its sign. Every negative timing difference selects potentiation, while every positive timing difference selects depression. The magnitude then comes from the selected coefficient and the current-weight factor. A rule with $t_j-t_i=-2\text{ ms}$ and one with $t_j-t_i=-10\text{ ms}$ use the same branch here.

**Question.** Why does a presynaptic spike followed by a postsynaptic spike strengthen the connection?

**Answer.** That order associates the incoming presynaptic event with the later postsynaptic spike. The timing difference satisfies $t_j-t_i\leq 0$, so the potentiation branch produces a positive weight update. Repeated pairings can make the presynaptic neuron increasingly influential at that synapse.

**Question.** Does a negative value of $t_j-t_i$ mean the weight change is negative?

**Answer.** The negative value describes spike order: the presynaptic time is earlier than the postsynaptic time. The branch selected by that negative timing difference uses $a_{+}$, producing a positive update. The sign of the timing difference selects the branch; the sign of its learning-rate coefficient determines whether the weight rises or falls.

The resulting mental model is a synapse carrying a small local clock comparison. It observes when an incoming spike arrives, compares that time with the receiving neuron's spike, and turns their order into a weight update. Pre-before-post timing leads to potentiation, post-before-pre timing leads to depression, and the current weight scales how large the adjustment becomes.
