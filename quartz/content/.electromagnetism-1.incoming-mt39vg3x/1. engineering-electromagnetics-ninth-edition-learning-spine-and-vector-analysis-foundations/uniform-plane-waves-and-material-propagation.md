# 1.375 Uniform Plane Waves and Material Propagation

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 599, Index entries: Free space, Good conductors, Page 600, Index entries: Loss tangent, Lossless medium, Lossy media, Penetration depth, Page 601, Index entries: Poynting vector, Poynting's theorem, Page 603, Index entry: Uniform plane wave, Page 604, Index entry: Wave propagation

Uniform plane-wave theory follows from Maxwell's equations and provides the basic propagation model used before reflection, waveguides, and antennas. In free space, the source develops the uniform-plane-wave equation, its solutions, the vector Helmholtz equation, intrinsic impedance, wavelength, phase constant, and real instantaneous field forms. A TEM wave has electric and magnetic fields transverse to the direction of propagation. Poynting's theorem and the Poynting vector describe electromagnetic power flow and wave power. Material propagation is divided into perfect dielectrics, lossless media, lossy media, good dielectrics, conducting media, and good conductors. Indexed quantities include propagation constant, wavenumber, loss tangent, gain or attenuation behavior, penetration depth, intrinsic impedance, and power density. In good conductors, the approximation leads to skin effect and skin-effect resistance. The microwave-oven entry provides an application of power absorption in material. This node supplies the wave impedance and power concepts needed for interface reflection, transmission, polarization, guided waves, and radiation.

## Core Ideas

- The uniform-plane-wave equation follows from the coupled electromagnetic field equations.
- Free-space solutions are characterized by phase constant, wavelength, wavenumber, and intrinsic impedance.
- TEM fields are transverse to the direction of propagation.
- The Poynting vector represents electromagnetic power-flow density.
- Loss tangent helps characterize dielectric loss.
- Lossy-media propagation includes both phase variation and attenuation.
- Good-dielectric and good-conductor approximations simplify the general lossy-medium expressions.
- Skin effect confines high-frequency conductor current and fields near the surface.
- Penetration depth measures field decay into a lossy material.
