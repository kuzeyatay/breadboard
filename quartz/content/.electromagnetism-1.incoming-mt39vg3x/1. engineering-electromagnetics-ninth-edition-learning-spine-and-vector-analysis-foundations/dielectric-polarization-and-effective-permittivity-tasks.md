# 1.76 Dielectric Polarization and Effective Permittivity Tasks

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 155, Chapter 5 Problems 5.27 through 5.30

Problems 5.27 through 5.30 develop the relation between molecular dipoles, polarization, electric flux density, and relative permittivity. A material containing $N$ dipoles per unit volume has polarization determined by the dipole moment per molecule and the number density. The constitutive identity $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P$ then connects polarization to permittivity and susceptibility. One task asks for the dielectric constant when electric flux density is four times polarization, while another uses a specified polarization field in a coaxial dielectric to recover $\mathbf D$, $\mathbf E$, voltage, susceptibility, and molecular dipole moment. The composite-material problem shows that, under its stated uniform-mixture assumptions, the relative permittivity is a number-fraction-weighted average: $\epsilon_r=f\epsilon_{r1}+(1-f)\epsilon_{r2}$. These exercises bridge microscopic polarization and macroscopic dielectric behavior.

## Core Ideas

- Polarization is dipole moment per unit volume.
- $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P$ separates vacuum and material contributions.
- Relative permittivity can be inferred from relationships among $\mathbf D$, $\mathbf E$, and $\mathbf P$.
- A spatially varying polarization produces corresponding spatially varying fields.
- The stated composite model gives a number-fraction-weighted permittivity.

## Source Anchors
