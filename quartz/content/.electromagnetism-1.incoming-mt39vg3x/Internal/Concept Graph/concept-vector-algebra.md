## ConceptNode: Vector Algebra

Planning node for [[vector-algebra|1.6 Vector Algebra]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

Vector addition follows the parallelogram law and may also be visualized by placing the tail of one vector at the head of another to form a triangle. Addition is commutative, $\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{A}$, and associative, $$\mathbf{A}+(\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{C})=(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})+\mathbf{C}.$$ Coplanar vectors can be added by resolving them into horizontal and vertical components, and the same method extends to three dimensions using three components. Subtraction is converted into addition by reversing the second vector: $\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{A}+(-\mathbf{B})$. Multiplying a vector by a positive scalar changes its magnitude without changing its direction, while multiplication by a negative scalar also reverses its direction. Division by a scalar means multiplication by its reciprocal. Two vectors are equal when their difference is zero. For vector fields, addition and subtraction are performed between vectors defined at the same spatial point.

### Key planning details

- Vector addition follows the parallelogram or head-to-tail construction.
- Vector addition is commutative and associative.
- Components along corresponding directions are added separately.
- Vector subtraction reverses the second vector before addition.
- Positive scalar multiplication preserves direction.
- Negative scalar multiplication reverses direction.
- Vector fields are combined point by point.
