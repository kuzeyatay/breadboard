## ConceptNode: Turnstile Antenna Polarization

Planning node for [[turnstile-antenna-polarization|1.341 Turnstile Antenna Polarization]].

Source: [[engineering-electromagnetics-9th-ed-9nbsped-compress|Engineering Electromagnetics, Ninth Edition: Learning Spine and Vector Analysis Foundations]]

Locations: Page 567, Problem 14.23

A turnstile antenna consists here of two identical crossed dipoles lying along the $x$ and $y$ axes in the $xy$ plane and fed at their common origin. Equal current amplitudes are supplied, with the $x$-directed antenna assigned zero reference phase. The polarization observed on the positive $z$ axis is then controlled by the relative phase $\xi$ of the $y$-directed antenna. Circular polarization requires orthogonal field components of equal magnitude in phase quadrature, with the sign of the quarter-cycle phase difference selecting the rotation sense under the text's phasor convention. Linear polarization along the $45^\circ$ axis requires equal in-phase orthogonal components so their vector sum points midway between the $x$ and $y$ axes. The reusable procedure is to write the two orthogonal radiated field phasors at the observation point, apply the relative phase to one component, and inspect the time-dependent tip of the resultant electric-field vector. This topic connects antenna feed phasing to polarization rather than to beam direction, distinguishing it from ordinary linear-array steering.

### Key planning details

- The turnstile uses two identical crossed dipoles along the $x$ and $y$ axes.
- The dipoles have equal current amplitudes and a common feed point.
- The $x$-directed dipole is the zero-phase reference.
- Equal orthogonal components in phase quadrature produce circular polarization.
- Equal orthogonal components in phase produce linear polarization at $45^\circ$.
- The sign of the quadrature phase determines polarization handedness under the adopted convention.

### Source coverage

- Problem 14.23 places two crossed dipoles in the $xy$ plane along the $x$ and $y$ axes.
- Both dipoles are fed at the origin with equal currents.
- The $x$-directed antenna has zero reference phase.
- The requested observations are made on the positive $z$ axis.
- The requested states are left circular polarization and linear polarization along the $45^\circ$ axis.
