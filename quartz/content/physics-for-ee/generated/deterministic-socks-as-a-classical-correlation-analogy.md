## Deterministic Socks as a Classical Correlation Analogy

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 13

The lecture uses ordinary mismatched socks to model a classical correlated system. In this analogy, Professor Bertlmann always wears one pink sock and one blue sock. If Alice sees that one sock is blue, Bob can immediately infer that the other sock is pink, but this is not a quantum effect because the colors were already real before observation. The socks are deterministic because there may be a fixed rule, such as wearing the pink sock on the right and the blue sock on the left on certain days. They are also local because their properties depend only on nearby circumstances, such as how they were stacked in the closet, not on a distant measurement. This example establishes the contrast between classical ignorance and quantum indefiniteness: in the classical case, measurement only reveals a preexisting property.

### Key points

- Classical mismatched socks are always one pink and one blue.
- Seeing one sock color lets the observer infer the other color.
- The socks are considered real because each sock already has a color before measurement.
- The system is deterministic because the outcome can be fixed by an underlying rule.
- The system is local because each sock's color depends on nearby preparation rather than distant observation.
- The observer's uncertainty is only lack of knowledge, not absence of a definite property.
