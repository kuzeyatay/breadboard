## Wave Packet Width and Fourier Uncertainty

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 15

The lecture closes by linking wave packet localization to a Fourier-style uncertainty relation between spatial width and wave-number spread. If the function in $k$-space is narrow, meaning the amplitudes are concentrated around a small range of wave numbers, the resulting pulse in solution or position space is broad. Conversely, a broad pulse in $k$-space produces a smaller, narrower pulse in the spatial domain. The lecturer identifies this as exactly how a Fourier integral works: a narrow pulse in one domain has a broad response in the conjugate domain, while a broad pulse has a narrower spectrum. In the quantum context, this means that having a well-localized particle in space requires a spread of wave numbers, while a sharply defined wave number produces poor localization.

### Key points

- A narrow function in $k$-space produces a broad pulse in solution or position space.
- A broad function in $k$-space produces a smaller pulse in the spatial domain.
- Wave number $k$ is related to wavelength.
- The amplitudes at different wavelengths determine the packet shape.
- The lecture identifies the relation as a property of Fourier integrals.
- A narrow pulse gives a broad spectrum.
- A broad pulse gives a narrower spectrum.
- The topic is connected to uncertainty in the wave packet description.
