## Schrodinger Equation as a Special Wave Equation

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 13

The lecture frames Schrodinger's equation as a special kind of wave equation for quantum particles. During the derivation, the required factors include $\hbar\omega$ and $\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$, connecting the wave form to energy and momentum-like quantities. The final time-dependent structure is stated with the imaginary unit multiplying $\hbar$ and the time derivative of the wave function: $i\hbar\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}$. The lecturer corrects an extra $\omega$ and emphasizes that this factor came from differentiation. The result is not a classical real-valued wave equation: its solutions are complex wave functions. This topic serves as the conceptual anchor for the later interpretation, because Schrodinger's equation determines the wave function whose squared magnitude is later used as a probability density.

### Key points

- The equation is presented as a wave equation, but a special one.
- The derivation requires factors such as $\hbar\omega$ and $\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$.
- The time-derivative side includes $i\hbar\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}$.
- The lecturer notes that the extra $\omega$ should be removed because it came from differentiation.
- Schrodinger's equation produces complex-valued wave functions.
- The wave function $\psi$ is the mathematical object solved for by the equation.
- The lecture says Schrodinger established this equation.
