## Wave Packet as Both Particle and Wave

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 15

The lecture uses the wave packet to illustrate how a quantum object can show both particle-like and wave-like aspects. In the particle sense, the packet is localized in space: it has a finite width $\Delta x$ over which the probability density has significant values, and it is likely to be found near the maximum of that density. In the wave sense, the packet still has periodic structure because it is built from contributions with different frequencies or wave numbers. The lecturer explicitly states that the wave packet still has aspects of both a particle and a wave. This dual description is not treated as a contradiction; it follows from the Schrodinger wave function and its probability interpretation.

### Key points

- The wave packet has localization in space like a particle.
- The finite region of significant probability is described by $\Delta x$.
- The particle is most likely found where the probability density is highest.
- The packet has wave-like periodic structure.
- The periodic structure comes from contributions over different frequencies or wave numbers.
- The lecture says the packet acts both like a particle and a wave.
- This behavior is determined by the wave function.
