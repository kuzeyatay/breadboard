## Reflection at Fixed Ends

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11

When a wave pulse reaches a fixed end, the reflected pulse is inverted: a positive displacement reflects as a negative displacement. The lecture explains this using Newton's third law. As the pulse reaches the wall, the string pulls upward on the wall; the wall exerts an equal and opposite downward reaction force on the string. This downward force excites the string in the opposite direction, flipping the sign of the pulse amplitude. A fixed end therefore behaves as a node, because the endpoint itself cannot move. This sign reversal is important when forming standing waves on strings fixed at their endpoints, because the reflected backward-traveling wave must carry the appropriate negative sign to satisfy the boundary condition.

### Key points

- A pulse reflecting from a fixed end flips its amplitude sign.
- The reflected pulse travels backward after inversion.
- The physical cause is the wall's reaction force on the string.
- Newton's third law gives action equals minus reaction.
- The fixed end cannot move, so it acts as a node.
- The sign flip affects the mathematical form of the backward-traveling wave.
