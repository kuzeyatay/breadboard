## Entanglement as Shared Quantum State

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

The lecture defines entanglement as a property of a quantum system describing how much multiple objects share a single state. Before measurement, the pair is not best understood as two separate objects with fixed individual states; instead, the objects form one combined quantum object with multiple possible outcomes. In the sock analogy, the pair is described as a single quantum object with two possible states, pink or blue, rather than as a left sock with one definite color and a right sock with another definite color. Measurement disentangles the system by forcing one definite result. This idea is central to the later examples involving photons, electron spins, and quantum computing, where entangled components share state information until measurement.

### Key points

- Entanglement describes multiple objects sharing a single quantum state.
- Before measurement, individual properties may be indefinite.
- The lecture describes the pair as one quantum object with two possible states.
- Measurement disentangles the objects by producing a definite outcome.
- Entanglement is presented as a core property of quantum mechanics.
- The same concept applies beyond the sock analogy to photons, spins, and qubits.
