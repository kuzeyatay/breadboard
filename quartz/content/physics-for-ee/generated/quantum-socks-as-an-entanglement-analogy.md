## Quantum Socks as an Entanglement Analogy

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 13, Page 14

The lecture introduces 'quantum socks' as an analogy for entangled quantum systems. Unlike deterministic socks, quantum socks do not have a definite color before measurement. Alice has a 50% chance of measuring pink and a 50% chance of measuring blue, and only after she measures one sock does the color become definite. Once one sock is measured, the other must be the opposite color, even if it is far away. This illustrates the key feature of entanglement: the pair behaves as a connected quantum system rather than as two objects with independently prewritten properties. The analogy is explicitly not literal, since socks are not quantum objects, but it is used to capture how measurement turns an indefinite shared state into correlated definite outcomes.

### Key points

- Quantum socks do not have a definite color before measurement.
- A measurement gives a 50% chance of pink and a 50% chance of blue.
- If one sock is measured pink, the other must be blue.
- If one sock is measured blue, the other must be pink.
- The pair is treated as connected by entanglement rather than by preexisting colors.
- The lecture clarifies that real socks are not quantum objects.
