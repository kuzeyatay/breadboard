## Standing Wave Trigonometric Form

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11

The lecture describes deriving a standing wave expression by superimposing a forward-traveling wave and a backward-traveling wave. At a fixed node, the reflected backward wave has a negative sign because reflection at a fixed end flips the amplitude. Adding the two traveling waves gives a sum of cosine terms, which can be rewritten using a trigonometric identity. The result separates into a spatial factor and a temporal factor: the spatial factor determines where nodes and antinodes occur, while the temporal factor determines how the allowed points oscillate in time. The spatial part must satisfy the boundary condition at the string ends. For fixed ends, sine-shaped spatial patterns fit because they have nodes at the endpoints, whereas cosine patterns generally do not satisfy the fixed-end boundary condition.

### Key points

- A standing wave can be written as the sum of a forward wave and a backward wave.
- At a fixed end, the backward-traveling wave includes a negative sign from reflection.
- The sum of cosines can be rewritten using a trigonometric identity.
- The standing wave expression separates into a spatial part and a temporal part.
- The spatial part must fit the endpoint boundary conditions.
- For fixed endpoints, sine patterns fit because they have nodes at the ends.
