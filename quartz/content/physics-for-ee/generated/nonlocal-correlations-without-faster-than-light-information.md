## Nonlocal Correlations Without Faster-Than-Light Information

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 13

The lecture emphasizes that entanglement involves instantaneous correlations but does not transmit usable information faster than light. If Alice measures one entangled object and obtains pink, the partner is immediately known to be blue; if she obtains blue, the partner is known to be pink. This remains true even if the objects are far apart, such as being sent in different directions through space and observed through telescopes. However, no controllable message is sent because Alice does not know in advance what outcome she will obtain. Information transfer would require controlling the measured result, such as choosing to measure pink so that the other becomes blue, but the quantum outcome is probabilistic. Therefore, the strange instantaneous update does not violate the rule that information propagates no faster than light.

### Key points

- Entangled outcomes are correlated instantaneously after measurement.
- The correlation can occur even when the two objects are very far apart.
- No faster-than-light message is sent because the measurement outcome is not known beforehand.
- Usable information would require control over which result appears.
- The lecture distinguishes instantaneous state update from information propagation.
- The example is extended to entangled photons with different measured colors.
