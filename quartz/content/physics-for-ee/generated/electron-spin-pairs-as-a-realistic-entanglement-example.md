## Electron Spin Pairs as a Realistic Entanglement Example

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 15

The lecture moves from the sock analogy to a more realistic example: two electrons originating from the same atom. The net spin of the atom must be zero, so if one electron is measured as spin up, the other must be spin down, and if one is measured spin down, the other must be spin up. Before measurement, the pair is not known to be in one arrangement or the other; the possible states are up-down and down-up. The two electrons form an entangled pair, so measuring particle one determines what will be found if particle two is measured afterward. This gives a physical version of the same complementary-outcome structure used in the sock analogy.

### Key points

- Two electrons from the same atom can form an entangled pair.
- The lecture states that the net spin has to be zero.
- If one electron is spin up, the other must be spin down.
- If one electron is spin down, the other must be spin up.
- Before measurement, the pair may be described by the alternatives up-down and down-up.
- Measuring one particle determines the correlated result for the second particle.
