## Diffraction Pattern Dependence on Slit Width

Source: [[979070-english|Diffraction, Interference, and Course Feedback]]

Locations: Page 17

The lecture emphasizes that the width of the central bright diffraction beam depends strongly on the ratio between wavelength and slit width. For the first minimum, the formula gives approximately $\theta \approx \lambda/a$ when $m=1$. If the slit width $a$ is comparable to the wavelength $\lambda$, the central bright beam becomes very wide. If the slit is much larger than the wavelength, the diffraction pattern narrows and most intensity is focused near the center. Therefore, wider slits produce narrower diffraction patterns, while smaller slits or larger wavelengths produce broader spreading. This also explains why diffraction is easier to observe when the wavelength is large enough compared with the object or aperture.

### Key points

- The central bright beam width is set by the first minimum.
- For $m=1$, the first minimum is approximately at $\theta \approx \lambda/a$.
- If $a$ is comparable to $\lambda$, the central beam is wide.
- If $a$ is much larger than $\lambda$, the central beam is narrow.
- Wider slits produce narrower diffraction patterns.
- Visible diffraction requires a wavelength large enough compared with the object or aperture.
