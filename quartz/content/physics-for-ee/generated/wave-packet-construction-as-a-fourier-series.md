## Wave Packet Construction as a Fourier Series

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

The lecture explains that setting up a localized quantum wave requires assigning amplitudes to different wave numbers. For every wave number, one can give a certain amplitude, producing an amplitude distribution over wave number. The lecturer compares this directly to a Fourier series for a particle. The resulting wave packet is therefore not a single wave but a superposition of many waves with different wave numbers and amplitudes. This explains why the packet can be localized in space while still retaining wave-like periodic structure. The Fourier viewpoint also prepares for the later claim that spatial width and wave-number spread are linked: the same mathematical property that relates a time-domain pulse to its spectrum also relates the spatial wave packet to its $k$-space distribution.

### Key points

- A wave packet is built by assigning amplitudes to wave numbers.
- The amplitude distribution is spread over wave number $k$.
- The lecture compares this construction to a Fourier series.
- The packet is a superposition of contributions from different wave numbers.
- An average wave number can be defined even when many wave numbers contribute.
- This construction explains how a localized pulse can still have wave-like structure.
- The Fourier-series idea leads into the position and wave-number width relation.
