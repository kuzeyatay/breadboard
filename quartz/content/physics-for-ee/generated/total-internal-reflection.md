## Total Internal Reflection

Source: [[977689-english|Refraction, Fermat's Principle, Apparent Images, and Total Internal Reflection]]

Locations: Page 13

Total internal reflection occurs when light traveling in a higher-refractive-index medium reaches an interface with a lower-refractive-index medium at an incident angle greater than the critical angle. The lecture describes the process gradually: at perpendicular incidence, reflection and refraction both occur but the geometry is not visually dramatic; as the angle increases, part of the light is reflected and part is refracted; at the critical angle, the refracted ray travels along the interface; beyond that angle, no ray propagates into the second medium and the light is reflected internally. The lecturer emphasizes that the course can calculate the angle at which total internal reflection begins, but not generally the exact fractions of light reflected and refracted. Those reflection and transmission amounts belong to a later electromagnetic treatment. Total internal reflection is then used as a practical concept for solving prism problems and understanding light confinement.

### Key points

- Total internal reflection requires light to start in the higher-index medium.
- The incident angle must exceed the critical angle.
- Below the critical angle, both reflected and refracted rays can exist.
- At the critical angle, a refracted ray propagates along the interface.
- Above the critical angle, no transmitted ray propagates into the second medium.
- The reflected ray remains in the original medium.
- The course can calculate critical-angle conditions but not full reflected and refracted intensities.
- The concept is used in prism and light-confinement problems.
