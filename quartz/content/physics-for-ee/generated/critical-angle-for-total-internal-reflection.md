## Critical Angle for Total Internal Reflection

Source: [[977689-english|Refraction, Fermat's Principle, Apparent Images, and Total Internal Reflection]]

Locations: Page 13

The critical angle is the incident angle at which the refracted ray no longer enters the second medium but instead travels along the interface. In the lecture, medium $A$ has refractive index $n_A$ and medium $B$ has refractive index $n_B$. Total internal reflection can occur only when light travels from a higher-index medium to a lower-index medium, so $n_A > n_B$. At the critical angle, the refracted angle is $\theta_B = 90^\circ$, so $\sin \theta_B = 1$. Starting from Snell's law, $n_A \sin \theta_A = n_B \sin \theta_B$, the critical condition gives $n_A \sin \theta_c = n_B$, hence $$\sin \theta_c = \frac{n_B}{n_A}.$$ This equation only has a valid solution when $n_B/n_A \leq 1$. For incident angles larger than $\theta_c$, the light is totally reflected back into medium $A$.

### Key points

- The critical angle occurs when the refracted ray is at $90^\circ$ to the normal.
- At the critical angle, the transmitted ray propagates along the interface.
- The derivation uses $\sin \theta_B = 1$.
- Snell's law gives $n_A \sin \theta_c = n_B$.
- The critical-angle formula is $\sin \theta_c = n_B/n_A$.
- A valid critical angle requires $n_B < n_A$.
- The sine constraint explains why total internal reflection cannot occur from lower to higher refractive index.
- Angles larger than the critical angle produce total internal reflection.
