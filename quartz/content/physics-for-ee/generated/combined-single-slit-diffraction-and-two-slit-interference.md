## Combined Single-Slit Diffraction and Two-Slit Interference

Source: [[979070-english|Diffraction, Interference, and Course Feedback]]

Locations: Page 17, Page 18

The lecture explains that real two-slit experiments often combine two effects. If each slit has finite width instead of being infinitesimally narrow, then each slit individually produces a single-slit diffraction pattern. At the same time, the pair of slits produces a two-slit interference pattern. The observed intensity is therefore an interference fringe pattern modulated by a broader single-slit diffraction envelope. The central wide bright region belongs to diffraction, while the finer repeated fringes come from interference between the two slits. The lecturer describes this as the diffraction envelope being combined with the interference pattern and notes that such real-life situations become complicated quickly, so understanding the individual effects is the main goal.

### Key points

- Finite-width slits produce single-slit diffraction individually.
- Two slits together produce two-slit interference.
- The observed pattern combines both effects.
- The diffraction pattern acts as an envelope over the interference fringes.
- The central wide bright region belongs to diffraction.
- The fine fringe structure comes from two-slit interference.
