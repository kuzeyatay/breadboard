## Bridge Resonance Example

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 12

The lecture uses a bridge as an example of standing-wave resonance in a real structure. People can excite a standing wave pattern on a bridge, including a third mode with three antinodes, if they drive the bridge at a suitable rhythm. This requires effort because the excitation must match the resonant behavior of the bridge. The example also explains why soldiers are instructed to march out of phase or out of step when crossing bridges. If a group marches in step, their periodic force can be in phase and may match a resonant frequency of the bridge, amplifying motion and potentially damaging it. Walking out of phase produces a more random excitation that is less likely to drive a strong resonance.

### Key points

- A bridge can support standing-wave-like resonant motion.
- The third harmonic on a bridge can show three antinodes.
- Strong excitation is needed to drive higher modes.
- In-phase rhythmic walking can excite bridge resonance.
- Soldiers should avoid marching in step across bridges.
- Random or out-of-phase walking reduces the risk of resonant amplification.
- Resonance can damage a bridge if the excitation frequency is unfavorable.
