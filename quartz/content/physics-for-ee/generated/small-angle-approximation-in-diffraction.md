## Small-Angle Approximation in Diffraction

Source: [[979070-english|Diffraction, Interference, and Course Feedback]]

Locations: Page 17

The lecture notes that for visible light, the wavelength is often much smaller than the slit width. In that case the ratio $\lambda/a$ is small, so the diffraction angles for the first few dark fringes are also small. This allows the approximation $\sin\theta \approx \theta$ when $\theta$ is measured in radians. Applying this to the single-slit formula turns $$\sin\theta = \frac{m\lambda}{a}$$ into the approximate angular relation $$\theta \approx \frac{m\lambda}{a}.$$ The lecturer presents this as a common approximation in physics literature and textbooks, especially when only conceptual understanding or approximate angular widths are needed.

### Key points

- Visible-light wavelengths are often much smaller than slit sizes.
- When $\lambda/a$ is small, diffraction angles are small.
- For small angles in radians, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$.
- The dark-fringe condition becomes $\theta \approx m\lambda/a$.
- The approximation is commonly used in physics books and literature.
- It is useful for estimating angular fringe positions.
