## Superposition of Traveling Pulses

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11

When two pulses travel through the same medium at the same time, their displacements add by superposition. In the lecture example, two pulses with different amplitudes pass through one another, producing a larger combined peak in the middle while overlapping. The pulses do not bounce off each other merely because they meet; instead, each continues propagating in its original direction after the overlap. Reflection requires a boundary condition such as a fixed end or a free end, not just the presence of another pulse. This illustrates that wave interaction in a linear medium is not like collision between objects: the medium displacement is temporarily the sum of the separate wave solutions, and the individual waves remain valid solutions.

### Key points

- Two overlapping pulses add their displacements while they occupy the same region.
- Different amplitudes make the superposition effect visually clear because the combined shape reflects both contributions.
- A pulse traveling right and a pulse traveling left pass through one another rather than reflecting from each other.
- Reflection occurs at a boundary such as a fixed end or free end, not at another pulse.
- The combined pulse shape during overlap is temporary; the separate pulses continue afterward.
