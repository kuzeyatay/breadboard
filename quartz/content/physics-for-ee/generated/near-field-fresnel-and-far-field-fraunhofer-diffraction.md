## Near-Field Fresnel and Far-Field Fraunhofer Diffraction

Source: [[979070-english|Diffraction, Interference, and Course Feedback]]

Locations: Page 16, Page 17

The lecture distinguishes between near-field and far-field diffraction. Near-field diffraction is identified as Fresnel diffraction, where the screen or observation point is not sufficiently far away for the simpler far-field geometry. The lecture then focuses on the far-field case, where a screen is far away and the interference can be analyzed through path differences between light rays from the slit. In the far field, the angular position $\theta$ becomes the key variable, and conditions for constructive or destructive interference can be written in terms of $\sin\theta$. This far-field pattern is referred to as Fraunhofer diffraction. The course prioritizes this simpler case because it supports the main conceptual understanding needed for single-slit diffraction.

### Key points

- Fresnel diffraction refers to the near-field diffraction case.
- Fraunhofer diffraction refers to the far-field diffraction pattern.
- The lecture focuses mainly on a distant screen and far-field considerations.
- Far-field analysis uses path differences between light rays from the slit.
- Angular position $\theta$ describes where bright and dark regions appear.
- The far-field case leads to a simple dark-fringe condition.
