## Quantum Correlations Exceeding Bell's Inequality

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 14

The lecture states that numerous experiments have confirmed quantum correlations exceeding Bell's inequality. This empirical result supports the view that entanglement is not merely an illusion produced by hidden predetermined properties. When the measured correlation value is larger than Bell's classical bound, the lecture treats entanglement as the only possible explanation. These experiments show that quantum systems can display correlations stronger than those allowed by the hidden-variable account described earlier. The result is presented as central to quantum mechanics: measurement on one part of an entangled system influences the description of another part, even when the objects are separated.

### Key points

- Experiments have measured correlations that exceed Bell's inequality.
- Exceeding Bell's bound supports genuine quantum entanglement.
- The result undermines the hidden-variable explanation presented in the lecture.
- Entanglement is described as essential to quantum mechanics.
- Measurement of one object influences the state assigned to another entangled object.
- The lecture connects this with the phrase 'spooky action at a distance'.
