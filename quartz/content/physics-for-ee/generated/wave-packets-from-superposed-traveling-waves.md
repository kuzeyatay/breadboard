## Wave Packets from Superposed Traveling Waves

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

To overcome the non-localization of a single traveling wave, the lecture introduces a sum of traveling waves that forms a localized pulse shape. Unlike a single sine or cosine extending to infinity, this pulse has a defined length in the $x$ direction and is approximately zero outside a finite range. The probability density of such a pulse is concentrated in a spatial region with width $\Delta x$, and the chance of finding the particle is highest where the probability density has its maximum. The lecturer describes the construction as a superposition of many waves, so an average wave number and average wavelength can still be assigned even though many wave numbers contribute. This localized superposition is called a wave packet.

### Key points

- A single traveling wave is not localized.
- A sum of traveling waves can generate a localized pulse.
- The pulse has a defined length in the $x$ direction.
- The pulse is approximately zero outside its finite spatial range.
- The probability density has a width $\Delta x$.
- The highest chance of finding the particle is where the probability density is maximal.
- The localized pulse is called a wave packet.
- A wave packet can have an average wave number and average wavelength.
