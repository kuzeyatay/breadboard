## Nodes and Antinodes

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11

Standing waves contain points that remain stationary and points that oscillate strongly. The stationary points are called nodes; the lecture notes that in Dutch they are called "knopen." Antinodes are positions where the amplitude is large, corresponding to the largest oscillation in the standing wave pattern. For a string fixed at both ends, the endpoints are nodes because they cannot move. In the fundamental harmonic there are nodes at both ends and one antinode between them. In the next higher harmonic, there is an additional node in the middle and two antinodes. These node and antinode patterns are directly tied to the length of the string and the number of half-wavelengths that fit between the fixed ends.

### Key points

- Nodes are parts of a standing wave that do not move.
- Antinodes are positions with large amplitude.
- Fixed endpoints are nodes in a string fixed at both ends.
- The fundamental harmonic has nodes at the ends and one antinode.
- The first higher harmonic has one node in the middle and two antinodes.
- The number and placement of nodes relate to the allowed wavelength and string length.
