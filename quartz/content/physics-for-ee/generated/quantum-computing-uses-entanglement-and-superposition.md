## Quantum Computing Uses Entanglement and Superposition

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 14, Page 15

The lecture identifies entanglement as being at the heart of quantum computing. Qubits use entanglement between electron spins, and the computer memory can be in a superposition of multiple states simultaneously. This does not make every calculation faster; the instructor specifically notes that only certain calculations benefit from this structure. The point is not to provide a detailed quantum computing model, but to show that the same principles of entanglement and superposition discussed in the lecture have technological relevance. The lecture also mentions that a master's track in electrical engineering and work at the DIFFER Institute on the TU/e campus focus on quantum computing.

### Key points

- Entanglement is central to quantum computing.
- Qubits can use entanglement between electron spins.
- Quantum computer memory can exist in a superposition of multiple states simultaneously.
- Superposition and entanglement can make certain calculations much quicker.
- Not all calculations benefit from quantum computing.
- The lecture mentions specialized study and research on quantum computing at TU/e.
