## Fraunhofer Diffraction Dark Fringe Formula

Source: [[979070-english|Diffraction, Interference, and Course Feedback]]

Locations: Page 17

For single-slit Fraunhofer diffraction, the lecture gives the locations of dark fringes as $$\sin\theta = \frac{m\lambda}{a}.$$ Here $a$ is the slit width, $\lambda$ is the wavelength, and $m=1,2,3,\dots$ labels the first, second, third, and later dark fringes. The first dark region occurs when there is a half-wavelength path difference between corresponding rays from paired parts of the slit, so that contributions cancel. The lecturer emphasizes that the diffraction index convention differs from interference: for diffraction dark fringes, counting starts at $m=1$, while earlier interference maxima started at $m=0$. This formula captures the angular positions of destructive interference in the far-field single-slit pattern.

### Key points

- Single-slit dark fringes occur at $\sin\theta = m\lambda/a$.
- $a$ denotes slit width and $\lambda$ denotes wavelength.
- $m=1$ gives the first dark fringe.
- $m=2$ gives the second dark fringe.
- The first minimum is associated with half-wavelength cancellation across paired slit regions.
- Diffraction fringe indexing starts differently from the interference maxima convention.
