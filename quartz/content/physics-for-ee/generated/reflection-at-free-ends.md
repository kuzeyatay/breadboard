## Reflection at Free Ends

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11

A wave reflecting from a free end does not invert its sign. The lecture describes a free end as a ring sliding on a pole, able to move up and down freely. Because the end is free to move vertically, there is no vertical reaction force from a wall that would reverse the displacement. Instead, the ring moves with the wave, and the reflected pulse keeps the same sign while traveling back in the opposite direction. This contrasts with a fixed end, where the immovable boundary supplies a reaction force that flips the pulse. Free-end reflection therefore preserves the pulse orientation and has a different boundary condition from fixed-end reflection.

### Key points

- A pulse reflecting from a free end does not flip sign.
- A free end can be modeled as a ring sliding freely on a pole.
- Because the endpoint can move vertically, there is no vertical reaction force from a wall.
- The reflected pulse travels backward with the same sign as the incident pulse.
- Free-end reflection contrasts with fixed-end reflection, where the pulse inverts.
- The boundary condition determines whether the reflected wave changes sign.
