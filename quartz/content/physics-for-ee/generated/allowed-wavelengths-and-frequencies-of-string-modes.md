## Allowed Wavelengths and Frequencies of String Modes

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 12

Once the fixed-end boundary condition is known, the allowed wavelengths for each mode can be found by solving $L = n\lambda/2$ for $\lambda$. This gives $$\lambda_n = \frac{2L}{n}.$$ In the fundamental mode, $n=1$, half a wavelength fits on the string, so the full wavelength is $2L$. The second mode fits one full wavelength along the string, and higher modes fit additional half-wavelengths. Because frequency and wavelength are related through the wave speed, these allowed wavelengths correspond to allowed resonant frequencies. The lecture also connects the frequency relation to the wave velocity on a string, written as $v = \sqrt{F/\mu}$, where $F$ is the string tension and $\mu$ is the linear mass density.

### Key points

- The fixed-end condition $L = n\lambda/2$ can be inverted to find allowed wavelengths.
- The wavelength of mode $n$ is $\lambda_n = 2L/n$.
- For the fundamental mode $n=1$, the wavelength is $2L$.
- The second mode fits a full wavelength on the string.
- Allowed wavelengths imply allowed frequencies through the wave speed relation.
- The wave speed on a string is given as $v = \sqrt{F/\mu}$.
- Mode number $n$ indexes the standing-wave harmonics.
