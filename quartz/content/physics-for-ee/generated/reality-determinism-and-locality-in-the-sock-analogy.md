## Reality, Determinism, and Locality in the Sock Analogy

Source: [[986217-english-1|Quantum Entanglement, Bell Inequality, and Course Summary]]

Locations: Page 13

The lecture contrasts classical and quantum systems through three properties: reality, determinism, and locality. Classical socks are real because they already possess definite colors before observation. They are deterministic because the color arrangement could be governed by a fixed underlying rule. They are local because a sock's property is determined by its immediate circumstances rather than a distant event. Quantum socks, by contrast, are described as not real in the classical sense because their color is not defined before measurement. They are nondeterministic because the outcome is a genuine 50/50 measurement result rather than a hidden predetermined color. They are nonlocal because the correlated outcome of the second object depends on measurement of the first object, even when separated.

### Key points

- Classical reality means properties exist before measurement.
- Classical determinism means outcomes are fixed by underlying rules.
- Classical locality means properties depend only on nearby surroundings.
- Quantum indefiniteness means the color is not assigned before measurement.
- Quantum nondeterminism means the 50/50 outcome is not explained by hidden predetermination.
- Quantum nonlocality means the measured result of one object is correlated with the state of the distant partner.
