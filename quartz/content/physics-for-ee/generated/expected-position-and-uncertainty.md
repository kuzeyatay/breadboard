## Expected Position and Uncertainty

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 14

The lecture introduces statistical quantities for the position of a quantum particle using the probability density derived from the wave function. The expected position is found by multiplying position $x$ by the probability density and integrating over all space. This expectation value is described as the expected outcome if one measures the particle's location. The lecture also introduces uncertainty in position through the variance of $x$. The variance is expressed conceptually as an integral involving $(x - \langle x \rangle)^2$ times the probability density, and the standard deviation is the square root of the variance. These definitions connect the probabilistic wave function picture to measurable averages and spreads in position measurements.

### Key points

- The expected position is computed from $x$ times the probability density integrated over all space.
- The integral runs from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$.
- The expected position corresponds to the expected outcome of a location measurement.
- Uncertainty in position is related to the variance of $x$.
- The variance uses $(x - \langle x \rangle)^2$ weighted by the probability density.
- The standard deviation is the square root of the variance.
- The lecturer emphasizes these are definitions for working with the probability density.
