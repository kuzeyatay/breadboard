## Normalization of Quantum Probability

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 14

The lecture reviews probability rules in the context of quantum position measurements. A probability $P$ for an event ranges from $0$ to $1$: $0$ means impossible and $1$ means certain. The chance of finding a particle at a position less than a given value $x_1$ is obtained by integrating the probability density from $-\infty$ to $x_1$. The probability of finding the particle somewhere in all space must equal $1$, because the particle has to be somewhere. This gives the normalization condition: the integral of the probability density from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$ equals $1$. The lecture points out that an infinitely extended free-particle sine or cosine wave cannot be normalized in this way because the integral would be infinite.

### Key points

- Probabilities range from $0$ to $1$.
- $P = 0$ means an event is not possible.
- $P = 1$ means an event is certain.
- The probability of finding a particle below $x_1$ comes from integrating the probability density from $-\infty$ to $x_1$.
- The total probability over all space must equal $1$.
- Normalization expresses that the particle must be somewhere.
- A sine or cosine wave extending infinitely cannot be normalized because its integral is infinite.
