## Complex Wave Functions and Real Measurements

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 13, Page 14

A major interpretive issue raised in the lecture is that Schrodinger wave functions are complex, while the world of measurements is real-valued. The lecturer notes that wave functions are always complex functions because the equation's solutions contain the imaginary number $i$. However, measured physical quantities are not observed as points on a complex plane. The resolution introduced here is that the wave function itself describes the state of the particle, while real probabilities come from the absolute value squared, $|\psi|^2$. Since $\psi$ is complex, $|\psi|^2$ is computed as the real part squared plus the imaginary part squared. This makes the probability density real and nonnegative, allowing a complex mathematical wave to produce real-valued predictions about where a particle may be found.

### Key points

- Schrodinger wave functions are complex functions.
- The source emphasizes that measured reality is real-valued, not complex-valued.
- The wave function describes the state of a particle.
- The measurable probability density is $|\psi|^2$.
- For a complex wave function, $|\psi|^2$ equals real part squared plus imaginary part squared.
- The probability density gives the density of finding a particle at a position.
- Complex wave functions become physically interpretable through real probability densities.
