## Standing Waves from Forward and Backward Traveling Waves

Source: [[974965-english-1|Standing Waves, Reflection, and Resonance on Strings]]

Locations: Page 11, Page 12

A standing wave can be produced by the superposition of a forward-traveling wave and a backward-traveling wave with the same wavelength and wave velocity. To an observer, the resultant pattern appears not to travel, even though physically there are still two traveling wave solutions present at the same time. This is the mechanism behind resonance on a string: a wave reflects from a boundary, travels back through the string, and interferes with the incident wave. When the frequency and wavelength match the allowed boundary conditions, the superposition creates a stable standing wave pattern with fixed nodes and oscillating antinodes. The lecture emphasizes that the standing wave is not a separate physical object replacing the traveling waves, but the sum of the forward and backward waves under the correct circumstances.

### Key points

- A standing wave results from a forward-traveling wave plus a backward-traveling wave.
- The two traveling waves must have exactly the same wavelength and wave velocity.
- The observed standing pattern is the resultant wave from superposition.
- Physically, the forward and backward traveling waves coexist as simultaneous solutions.
- Resonance on a string works through reflection and interference of traveling waves.
- Correct frequency and boundary conditions are required to form stable standing waves.
