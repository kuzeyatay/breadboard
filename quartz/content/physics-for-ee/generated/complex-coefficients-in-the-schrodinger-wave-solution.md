## Complex Coefficients in the Schrodinger Wave Solution

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 13

The derivation attempts to make a sine-and-cosine wave form satisfy the required factors in Schrodinger's equation. After comparing the coefficients in front of the sine and cosine terms, the lecture obtains two conditions: $A = -CB$ and $B = CA$. Substituting one condition into the other gives $A = -C^2 A$, so $C^2 = -1$. This means the constant $C$ must be the square root of $-1$, so the imaginary number is required. The convention used in the lecture is $C = i$, not $j$. This is the mathematical point where complex numbers enter the wave solution: if $B = iA$, then the solution can be written as an amplitude times a cosine plus $i$ times a sine. The lecturer emphasizes that complex numbers are not an optional decoration; they are required for the sine and cosine amplitudes to match correctly in the equation.

### Key points

- Matching sine and cosine amplitudes requires coefficient conditions.
- The lecture obtains $A = -CB$ and $B = CA$.
- Substitution gives $A = -C^2A$.
- For a nonzero amplitude, the condition becomes $C^2 = -1$.
- The constant $C$ is therefore chosen as the imaginary number $i$.
- The lecture explicitly notes the convention is to use $i$, not $j$.
- With $C = i$, the wave solution becomes proportional to $\cos(\text{argument}) + i\sin(\text{argument})$.
