## Complex Exponential Form of Schrodinger Solutions

Source: [[983068-english-1|Schrodinger Equation, Wave Functions, and Probability Interpretation]]

Locations: Page 13

Once the coefficient condition forces the use of $i$, the wave solution takes the form $A[\cos(\text{argument}) + i\sin(\text{argument})]$. The lecture identifies this as a familiar structure from Euler's formula, allowing the solution to be written as a complex exponential. In this context, complex exponentials are not merely a convenient mathematical shorthand; they are solutions to Schrodinger's equation. The source also describes a free particle wave function whose spatial part extends across all space. The general form can be separated into time behavior and spatial behavior, indicating that a wave function may be written as a product of factors that describe evolution in time and variation in space. This complex exponential representation becomes the bridge between wave-like mathematical descriptions and the later probability interpretation.

### Key points

- The solution has the form $A(\cos(\text{argument}) + i\sin(\text{argument}))$.
- Euler's formula allows this expression to be written as a complex exponential.
- The lecture states that complex exponentials solve Schrodinger's equation.
- The wave function can separate into time behavior and spatial behavior.
- For a free particle with definite momentum, the spatial wave extends across all space.
- The complex exponential form introduces both real and imaginary parts into the wave function.
