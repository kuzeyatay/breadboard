## Summary

This lecture chunk explains quantum entanglement using the analogy of Professor Bertlmann's mismatched socks. Classical deterministic socks have definite colors before observation, are locally determined, and merely reveal preexisting correlations when measured. Quantum socks, by contrast, represent entangled quantum objects whose individual properties are indefinite before measurement, become definite only upon measurement, and show correlations that appear instantaneously across distance without transmitting usable information faster than light. The lecture introduces the hidden-variable debate and John Stewart Bell's correlation experiment, where violations of Bell's inequality support quantum entanglement over local hidden-variable explanations. It also connects entanglement to wave function collapse, photon polarization experiments, electron spin pairs with net zero spin, and quantum computing with qubits in superpositions of multiple states. The final page summarizes the larger lecture sequence on wave functions, the one-dimensional Schrödinger equation, finite square wells, quantum oscillators, tunneling, and entanglement.
