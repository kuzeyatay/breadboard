Receiver Output Signal-to-Noise Ratio in PCM

Source: Communications 1 Course Reader - Sampling, PAM, PCM, and Noise

Locations: Page 46, Page 47, Page 48

After digitization and transmission, the quality of the reconstructed analog signal is measured using output signal-to-noise ratio, denoted (S/N)out​. The text defines output noise as any deviation of the reconstructed waveform from the original analog input. Two mechanisms contribute to this degradation: quantization errors introduced by rounding at the transmitter and bit errors introduced by channel noise or receiver impairment. Under the assumptions used in the chapter, the output SNR is expressed as (NS​)out​=1+4Pe​(M2−1)M2​, where M is the number of quantization levels and Pe​ is the bit-error probability. The peak output SNR is similarly (NS​)pk,out​=1+4Pe​(M2−1)3M2​. When Pe​≈0, quantization dominates and these reduce to (S/N)out​=M2 and (S/N)pk,out​=3M2. With M=2n, this yields the 6-dB rule in decibels: (NS​)out,dB​=6.02n+α, with α=0 dB for average SNR and 4.77 dB for peak SNR. The rule states that adding one bit per sample improves output SNR by about 6 dB, as long as bit errors are negligible and the signal distribution is uniform over the quantizer range.

5.4 Signal-to-noise ratio (after signal reconstruction) Once a signal has been digitized we would like to estimate the resulting quality of the reconstructed signal after reception. A typical way to estimate the quality of a signal is to evaluate the ratio between signal power and noise. This is often referred to as the Signal- to-noise ratio (SNR). Specifically in the case of signal reconstruction, we will define the SN Rout as the relationship between the original analog signal before digitization and the reconstructed waveform at the output of the receiver. Any error in the reconstruction, and hence a deviation from original signal, will be considered as noise. Key points

  • Output SNR compares the original analog signal to the reconstructed receiver output.
  • Two noise mechanisms are emphasized: quantization noise and bit errors.
  • The output SNR formula is 1+4Pe​(M2−1)M2​.
  • The peak output SNR formula is 1+4Pe​(M2−1)3M2​.
  • If Pe​≈0, output SNR depends only on quantization levels.
  • Using M=2n leads to the 6-dB rule.
  • Each added quantization bit improves SNR by about 6 dB when bit errors are negligible.
