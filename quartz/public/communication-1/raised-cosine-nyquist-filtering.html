Raised-cosine Nyquist filtering

Source: Communications 1 Course Reader - Sampling, PAM, PCM, and Noise

Locations: Page 92, Page 93

Raised-cosine filtering is presented as the practical pulse-shaping method used to minimize ISI in real bandwidth-limited systems. Its frequency response is flat up to f1​, rolls off smoothly between f1​ and the total bandwidth B, and is zero outside that range. The text defines the key parameters fΔ​=B−f0​,f1​=f0​−fΔ​,r=f0​fΔ​​,B=f0​(1+r), where f0​ is the 6-dB bandwidth and r is the rolloff factor. When r=0, the filter becomes an ideal rectangular filter; larger r values widen the bandwidth but improve time-domain concentration and reduce ISI sensitivity. The time-domain pulse is given by he​(t)=2f0​sinc(2f0​t)[1−(4fΔ​t)2cos(2πfΔ​t)​], and the maximum zero-ISI symbol rate is Dmax​=2f0​. The chapter uses visual examples to show that raised-cosine-filtered pulses fit a bandlimited channel much better than raw rectangular pulses, yielding received waveforms that preserve symbol values at their sampling instants.

Key points

  • Raised-cosine filtering is used to generate pulses that minimize ISI.
  • The transfer function has a flat region, cosine rolloff region, and zero region.
  • The rolloff factor is r=fΔ​/f0​.
  • Total bandwidth is B=f0​(1+r).
  • The inverse Fourier transform gives the time-domain raised-cosine pulse.
  • The maximum no-ISI symbol rate is Dmax​=2f0​.
  • Larger rolloff generally increases bandwidth but improves practical pulse behavior.
