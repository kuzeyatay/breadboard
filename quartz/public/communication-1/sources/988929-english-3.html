Summary

This chunk is a lecture-style introduction to a communication systems course, mixing course logistics with foundational technical ideas. The instructors explain how student feedback is used to change teaching methods, especially replacing in-class quiz enforcement with structured pre-lecture and entry quizzes, recaps, and clearer module guidance. The course centers on how analog information is converted into digital form, transmitted through channels such as wires, radio, or fiber, and reconstructed at the receiver. Core technical stages named in the lecture include sampling, quantization, coding, modulation or signaling, transmission, error correction, decoding, and waveform reconstruction. The lecture also introduces the idea that communication signals can be analyzed in both time and frequency, motivating Fourier series as a way to decompose periodic signals into orthogonal sinusoidal components. Several concrete examples are used, including audio transmission from microphone to loudspeaker, bit errors in a binary word, square-wave reconstruction from harmonics, and the sinc-shaped envelope of Fourier coefficients. The course structure includes a midterm, final exam, graded lab quizzes, optional mini-labs in MATLAB, a reader, video recordings, quizzes, and Discord-based interaction.
