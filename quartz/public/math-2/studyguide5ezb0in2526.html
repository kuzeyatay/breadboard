Introduction In the course Mathematics 2 (5EZB0), the notions of differentiation and integration that were introduced in the course Calculus (2WBB0) for functions of one variable are extended to the multi-variable case. Focus is on vector analysis in the 3-D case and the investigation of vector fields in this setting. The results have important applications in physics and engineering. For electrical engineers, a good understanding of the notion of vector fields and the ability to use appropriate techniques from vector analysis is indispensable in, for instance, the study of electro magnetics. The course addresses the following topics:

  • Curves, surfaces and parametrizations
  • Functions of several variables; partial derivatives
  • Multiple integration
  • Vector fields, line and surface integrals, flux
  • Notions of gradient, divergence and curl This study guide describes the contents of the course Mathematics 2 (5EZB0). The course is based on parts of the following book: R.A. Adams and C. Essex: Calculus - A complete course, Tenth Edition, Pearson Canada Inc., 2022. ISBN: 978-0-13-573258-8 This study guide is an addition to the book. First of all, it indicates which parts of the book will be discussed, and which exercises have to be made. If necessary, it will provide additional subject matter, consisting of both study material and additional exercises. In the schedule on the next pages, you can find the dates of the lectures (in principle twice a week, but in weeks 2,3,4 and 6 only once, due to holidays), the instructions (once or twice a week) and the studio classroom session (in weeks 2,6,8). We expect that you attend all these educational activities on Monday mornings and Thursday afternoons. In the remainder of this study-guide, the material is divided into 10 sections; each section describes the contents of one course week (we have 9 weeks during the quartile). Section 10 describes the details for the final test. Each section starts with a list of the educational activities in that week. After this list, (some of) the following subsections will appear:
  1. Subjects The first section of each chapter contains a list of the subjects to be treated from the book (Adams ). You have to read the indicated sections in detail, and study them carefully. In the lectures, the subject material will be introduced and elaborated, to ensure a quick and smooth entry to the new material. The lectures will be recorded, so if necessary you can follow the lecture online.
  2. Exercises Here the exercises are described that we expect you to do in the corresponding week. They are divided into preparation exercises, that are rather basic, and that you should be able to do yourself at home, and instruction exercises, that are more challenging, and for which you probably need some guidance or help. During the instruction, you can ask questions on the theory and all exercises. We will try to answer your questions on an individual basis; for this reason there will be an additional student-assistant (TA) in each room. If there are many students with the same problem/question, the instructor will explain a solution on the blackboard. The instructors will also do selected problems on the blackboard every class. In mastering mathematics, training your mathematical skills by making exercises is extremely important. For this reason, your presence and attendance in the in- structions is essential for a successful accomplishment of the course. Our goal is to let you work independently on an individual basis or in a small group of students, and to provide help and additional explanations at the moment that you need it. If you attend the exercise classes on a regular basis, you will experience that this is a rather efficient way of studying and that it really helps you to master the subject. Please note that the 2(+2) hours of instruction per week may not be enough to finish all weekly exercises. We expect that you need to spend 7 hours of self study per week, for studying the book, and to finish all exercises. In addition to the lectures and instructions, there will be studio classroom sessions on three Thursday afternoons. In these sessions you will work on problems related to automotive technology and/or electrical engineering, in which you will apply the mathe- matical techniques that are discussed in the course. This will mainly relate to the Physics for EE/AT course (5EPD0/5EPE0) that you took last quartile, or the Electromagnetics I course (5EPF0) that you will take next year. You will experience why mathematics is so important for automotive and electrical engineers, and at the same time improve both your engineering and mathematical skills. The problems that are discussed during this studio classroom session are not contained in this study guide. Instead they will be provided to you on a weekly basis through slide decks. There will be a question on a topic covered in a studio classroom session in the final exam. Assessment The assessment of the course consists of three components. (i) (Bi)-weekly electronic exercises. The electronic exercises have to be made at home. We use the platform SOWISO for this. Starting in week 2, five small electronic tests will be made available in SOWISO, approximately on a bi-weekly basis. You need to log into the system, and make the weekly electronic tests before the indicated deadline. ii

On Canvas we will announce when a SOWISO test is made available, and the exact deadline for that test. In SOWISO, you can take the tests as many times as you want within the given timeframe. After each attempt, you will be able to see the answers for that attempt. After submitting your answers, your test will be automatically graded. The best grade for each test of all attempts will be the grade corresponding to that test. This grade will contribute to your grade for the electronic exercises: the weighted mean of your scores for all graded electronic tests determines 10% of your final grade for the course. (ii) Midterm test on Thursday 21 May 2026, 15:45-17:15 h. This will be test in ANS with mutiple choice and/or open exercises. The grade of the midterm determines 10% of your grade for the course. There are NO resits of the Midterm test. (iii) Final test on Monday 22 June 2026, 9:00-12:00 h. The final test is an on-campus written test in ANS. Your grade for the final test determines 80% of your grade for the course. An additional requirement for passing the course is that your grade for the final test must be at least 5.0. If your grade of the final test is less than 5.0 you fail the course, and your grade will be ‘NVD’. In that case you can participate in the resit of the final test, which is scheduled on Monday 10 August 2026, 9:00-12:00 h. The exact subject matter of the midterm and final test will be announced in due time. Note: this study guide should be considered as work in progress and it is updated and extended during the quartile. Always check the latest version for your homework and tutorial exercises, information regarding additional study material, tests and exams, etc. If you notice typos or other mistakes in this study guide, please report them by e-mail to the lecturer of the course.
