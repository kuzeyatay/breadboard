Summary

This lecture segment explains refraction through analogies, observations, and applications. It uses a lifeguard running and swimming problem to illustrate that light follows the path of least time when traveling through media with different speeds or refractive indices. It explains apparent image displacement in water, where a fish appears higher than it really is because rays bend away from the normal when leaving water for air. The material also describes atmospheric refraction at sunset, where rays from different parts of the Sun bend by different amounts, making the Sun appear flattened. The final topic is total internal reflection, which occurs when light travels from a higher refractive index medium to a lower refractive index medium at angles above the critical angle. The critical angle is derived from Snell’s law using the condition θB​=90∘, giving sinθc​=nB​/nA​. Applications include optical fibers, laser light trapped in water streams, bicycle reflectors, prisms, and the sparkle of diamonds due to their high refractive index and small critical angle.
