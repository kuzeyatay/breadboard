Summary

This lecture chunk derives the need for complex-valued solutions when constructing a wave solution to Schrodinger’s equation for a one-dimensional particle. Matching sine and cosine coefficients leads to a constant whose square equals −1, so the imaginary number i is introduced and the wave solution becomes a complex exponential through Euler’s formula. The Schrodinger equation is presented as a special wave equation whose wave functions are generally complex even though measured physical quantities are real. The wave function ψ describes the state of a particle, while ∣ψ∣2 gives the probability density for finding the particle at a position. The lecture explains normalization, probability integrals, expectation value, variance, and standard deviation for particle position. It contrasts a non-localized single traveling wave with a localized wave packet built from a superposition of traveling waves. The final section connects localization and uncertainty to Fourier behavior: a narrow distribution in wave-number space produces a broad spatial pulse, while a broad wave-number distribution produces a narrow spatial pulse.
