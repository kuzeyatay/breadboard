Summary

This lecture segment explains diffraction using everyday examples such as phone pixels and CDs, where small repeating structures create visible diffraction or rainbow-like patterns. It develops single-slit diffraction from the idea that every point in a slit can act as a Huygens source, producing constructive and destructive interference in the far field. The Fraunhofer single-slit condition for dark fringes is given as sinθ=mλ/a, with m=1 marking the first dark fringe. The lecture emphasizes that diffraction patterns widen when the slit width is comparable to the wavelength and narrow when the slit is much larger than the wavelength. It also explains that real two-slit experiments combine single-slit diffraction envelopes with two-slit interference fringes. The final pages shift to a Mentimeter quiz about the optional midterm test, course resources, lecture attendance, recordings, commute time, and desired improvements to lectures and instruction sessions.
